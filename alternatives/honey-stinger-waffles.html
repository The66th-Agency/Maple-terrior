Why Riders in Canada Look for an Alternative

The first reason is where you can buy them. Honey Stinger's product pages say its "website sales currently serve US and US territories," and send buyers in Canada to stores (Honey Stinger Honey Energy Waffles). Its list of products in Canadian stores names 14 waffle products, so which flavor you find depends on the shop. That works when you need a few waffles for this weekend. It is harder when you want a season's worth delivered.

The second reason is price. A box of 16 Honey waffles is $25.49 US on Honey Stinger's website. At the Bank of Canada's daily exchange rate for October 28, 2026, 1.4036 Canadian dollars to the US dollar, that is $35.78 Canadian, or $2.24 a waffle. Our caddy of 35 is $39.99, or $1.14 a waffle. Store prices in Canada are set by each store, so check the shelf price against these.

The third reason is taste. If you ride on honey waffles every weekend, a different filling is welcome on hour three. Ours is sweetened with pure maple syrup, and the five flavors are built on it.

Maple Syrup or Honey

Honey Stinger's Honey waffle lists organic wheat flour, organic palm oil, organic brown rice syrup, organic sugar and organic honey as its first five ingredients, and its product pages describe its waffles as certified organic. Honey Stinger says it uses honey in all of its products because it is "easy on the digestive system" (Honey Stinger, about the company).

Our stroopwafels start from Canadian-grown flour, with Canadian butter and eggs, and they are sweetened with glucose syrup, cane sugar and pure maple syrup, in the order the label lists them. They are non-GMO and kosher, with no artificial flavors and no preservatives. They are not certified organic. If you are curious how the two sweeteners differ, our guide to maple syrup vs honey compares them side by side.

Flavors, Organic and Gluten-Free

Honey Stinger has the wider range. Its US website lists 11 full-size waffle flavors in three lines: Honey, Vanilla and Chocolate in the classic line; Cookies & Cream, Salted Caramel and Cinnamon in the gluten-free line; and Almond Butter Maple and four peanut butter flavors in the nut butter line. It also sells Honey and Vanilla minis (all Honey Stinger energy waffles).

We make five flavors, each on the same maple filling: Pure Maple, Chocolate Maple, Strawberry Maple, Blueberry Maple and Matcha Maple. We also make one mini, the maple mini stroopwafels, 15 to a 128 g box. Every one contains wheat, and our stroopwafels may contain dairy, eggs, soy and nuts. If you need a gluten-free waffle, Honey Stinger's gluten-free line is the better buy.

  • You want a nut butter filling for a little more fat, as in Almond Butter Maple.

What Switching Involves

Switching is a swap of one waffle for another, so there is little to change. Five things are worth knowing before you order.

  • One for one. Our waffle is 34 g against Honey Stinger's 30 g, so one of ours takes the place of one of theirs in your plan, with 2 g more carbohydrate.
  • Test it in training first. Honey Stinger gives the same advice for its own waffles: "Always try the product in a training session before introducing a new product during competition" (Honey Stinger, why waffles are the perfect fuel). Our box of four, $8.99, covers a few training rides.
  • Each one is wrapped. Every stroopwafel in a caddy is wrapped on its own, so it rides in a jersey pocket or a frame bag. Coby C., quoted above, finds the wrappers "perfect for long bike rides."
  • Check the allergens. Ours contain wheat and may contain dairy, eggs, soy and nuts. There is no gluten-free version.
  • Plan for the caddy. A caddy holds 35, about two months of riding at four waffles a week. Follow the storage note on the package.
