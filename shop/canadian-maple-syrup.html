How to Tell Real Canadian Maple Syrup

Three checks on the label separate pure maple syrup from a table syrup that only looks the part, and the price is a fourth. Our guide to pure maple syrup and table syrup goes through each label in detail.

1Read the ingredients

Pure maple syrup lists one ingredient: maple syrup. A table syrup lists glucose or corn syrup and flavoring.

2Look for the grade

Retail maple syrup in Canada carries Canada Grade A and a color class such as Golden, Delicate Taste. Our maple syrup grades guide explains each one.

3Find the country

A Canadian bottle says so. Ours reads Product of Canada, in English and French.

Check the price

In our price index, pure maple syrup had a median of $2.76 per 100 mL against $0.54 for table syrup. Even the cheapest pure syrup, at $1.57, cost 2.8 times the pricier table syrup.

Questions About Canadian Maple Syrup

Canada Grade A is the federal grade on retail maple syrup in Canada. To carry it, the syrup must have 66 to 68.9 percent soluble solids, be free of fermentation, be even in color with no sediment or cloudiness, and taste of maple the way its color class should.

Quebec makes most of it, and Ontario, New Brunswick, Prince Edward Island and Nova Scotia make the rest, according to Maple from Canada. Ours comes from one farm.

Keep a bottle in the fridge once you open it, and keep unopened bottles in a cool, dry place.
