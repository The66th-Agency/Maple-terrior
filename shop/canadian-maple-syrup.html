The Four Canadian Grades, and the Two We Sell

Maple syrup sold as Canada Grade A is sorted into four color classes by how much light passes through it. The class tells you the taste, and all four meet the same Grade A standard.

On pancakes

Pour Golden for a light, delicate stack, and Dark when you want the maple to come through the butter and berries. The pancake guide explains why.

In baking

Bake with Dark, the stronger of our two grades, so you still taste the maple after the oven. The baking guide has the detail.

In coffee

Stir Golden into black coffee and Dark into a latte, one to two teaspoons a cup, as the coffee guide suggests.

Questions About Canadian Maple Syrup

Canada Grade A is the federal grade on retail maple syrup in Canada. To carry it, the syrup must have 66 to 68.9 percent soluble solids, be free of fermentation, be even in color with no sediment or cloudiness, and taste of maple the way its color class should.

Quebec makes most of it, and Ontario, New Brunswick, Prince Edward Island and Nova Scotia make the rest, according to Maple from Canada. Ours comes from one family farm.

Keep a bottle in the fridge once you open it, and keep unopened bottles in a cool, dry place.
