The Treat

What Maple Candy Actually Is

A confection with one defining quality. Maple candy carries the flavour of the syrup it is made from, which means the source matters as much as the method.

01

Pure Maple Syrup, Set Firm

Traditional maple candy is made by boiling pure maple syrup until the sugars begin to crystallise, then pouring it into moulds to set. No fillers, no corn syrup, no added flavour. The candy tastes the way the syrup tastes.

02

Shaped Like the Leaf It Comes From

The maple leaf is the traditional shape, pressed from soft moulds while the candy is still warm. Each piece is wrapped on its own, so it keeps its form and its freshness until you unwrap it.

03

Only as Good as Its Source

Because maple candy is almost entirely maple syrup, a blended or anonymous syrup shows up in the taste. Maple Terroir makes its candy from single-origin Quebec syrup, so every piece carries one farm's flavour.

The Method

How Maple Candy Is Made

01

Boil

Pure maple syrup is heated well past the point of syrup, until the water cooks off and the sugars concentrate. This is the same syrup we bottle, taken several degrees hotter.

02

Stir

The hot syrup is cooled, then stirred. Stirring is the step that turns clear syrup into the soft, pale candy that melts on the tongue.

03

Mould and Wrap

While it is still warm, the candy is pressed into maple leaf moulds and left to set. Each piece is then wrapped on its own to hold its shape and its freshness.

What to Expect

One Ingredient, Pure Maple Taste

Maple candy gives you the flavour of the syrup in a form you can carry in a pocket. Here is what a piece is actually like.

Melts, Does Not Crunch

Each piece is soft and semi-firm, and it softens further as it warms in your mouth, releasing a deep, caramel-edged maple flavour.

Naturally Gluten-Free

Pure maple syrup carries no gluten and no dairy, so traditional maple candy made from it is naturally free of both. If you have a severe allergy, check the label first.

A Canadian Classic

Families have made maple candy in Canadian sugar shacks for generations. It travels well and keeps its shape, which is why it has long been a favourite gift and souvenir.

Keeping It Fresh

How to Store Maple Candy

Because maple candy is made from pure maple syrup and little else, only air and warmth change it. Keep both out and it keeps well.

Day to day

Cool, Dry, and Wrapped

Keep the candy in its wrapper or an airtight container, in a cool, dry spot out of direct sunlight. Stored this way, it holds its texture for several weeks.

For longer

Refrigerate or Freeze

To keep maple candy for months, seal it well and refrigerate or freeze it. Let it come back to room temperature before unwrapping so it does not draw moisture.
