How Our Chocolate Covered Cherries Taste

Each piece is two layers, and you meet them in order. The drawing shows one cut in half.

1

The dark chocolate shell

Semi-sweet dark chocolate, the outside of every piece. It is the first thing you taste and the reason the cherry stays tidy in your hand.

2

A chewy dried cherry

Under the chocolate is a dried Canadian cherry. It is chewy, like a raisin, rather than juicy, so nothing runs out when you bite.

3

Maple in the sweetness

The cherries are sweetened with pure maple syrup before they are coated, so the maple sits in the fruit rather than on top of it. Cherry and dark chocolate lead; maple rounds them off.

Dried Cherries or Cherry Cordials

Search for chocolate covered cherries and two different sweets come up under one name. A cherry cordial is a single cherry around a liquid centre, and many home recipes wrap a maraschino cherry in fondant before dipping it. Ours are the other kind: small dried cherries coated in chocolate and eaten by the handful.

Are Chocolate Covered Cherries Healthy?

They are a treat, and the label says so plainly. The nutrition panel lists one serving as 100 g, which is the whole box: 440 calories, 56 g of sugars and 13 g of saturated fat.

The fruit adds something too. The same box has 7 g of fibre, 28 percent of the daily value, and 40 percent of the daily value for iron. Health Canada's guide to the percent daily value reads 5 percent or less as a little and 15 percent or more as a lot, and names fibre and iron among the nutrients to get more of, and saturated fat and sugars among those to limit.

Shared four ways, each share is 110 calories and 14 g of sugars.

How to Store Chocolate Covered Cherries

Follow the storage note on the box first. For chocolate in general, Iowa State University Extension gives four rules, and they suit these too.

18 to 21°C

Room temperature, 65 to 70°F, with the humidity under 50 to 55 percent.

Dark

A pantry or cupboard, because light, like air, makes chocolate oxidize and lose flavour.

Sealed

Once the box is open, move what is left to an airtight container. Cocoa butter takes on the smell of whatever sits near it.

Not the fridge

The fridge leaves moisture on the chocolate when it comes out. Use it only in real heat, wrapped tightly.

If the chocolate turns dusty grey, that is bloom: cocoa butter or sugar rising to the surface after heat or damp. The same Extension article says bloom changes the look only, not the taste, and the chocolate is still safe to eat.
