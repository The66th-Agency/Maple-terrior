Packing Maple for the Flight Home

CATSA, the authority that screens passengers at Canadian airports, lists maple syrup as a liquid, so where each souvenir travels depends on what it is.

  • In your carry-on

    Cookies, stroopwafels, chocolates, nuts, popcorn and tea are solid food, and CATSA lets solid food through security in a carry-on as well as a checked bag. See what fits

  • In your checked bag

    In a carry-on, CATSA allows syrup only in containers of 100 mL or less, packed with your other liquids in one clear 1 L bag, and treats maple spreads as gels under the same limit. Our 250 mL bottles and 160 g jar of maple cream go in the checked bag. Shop maple syrup

  • Or not in your luggage at all

    Buying for someone who lives in Canada? Type their address under Shipping at checkout and we send the order straight to them. Shop gift sets

From the Canadian Air Transport Security Authority's pages on maple syrup and liquids and non-solid food, checked October 3, 2026. Check them again before you fly.
