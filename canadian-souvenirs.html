What Goes in the Carry-On, and What Goes in the Checked Bag

Airport security treats maple syrup as a liquid, so where a souvenir travels depends on what it is. These are the rules from Canada's airport security authority.

Solid gifts go in either bag

Carry-on

Cookies, stroopwafels, chocolates, nuts, popcorn and tea are solid food, which CATSA allows in carry-on and checked bags.

Syrup over 100 mL goes in the hold

Checked bag

In a carry-on, CATSA allows maple syrup only in containers of 100 mL or less, all in one 1 L clear bag, and treats maple cream as a gel under the same limit. Pack a 250 mL bottle in your checked bag, wrapped in clothing.

Why Maple Is the Canadian Souvenir

A good souvenir is something the place is known for and easy to carry home or ship. Canada makes about three quarters of the world's maple syrup, according to Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada's 2020 industry overview, and Quebec made 90 percent of Canada's syrup in 2025, according to Statistics Canada.

Our syrup comes from one farm in Quebec's Appalachian Mountains. The organic syrups are certified by Ecocert, Canada Organic and USDA Organic, and Wayne and Kaori Lytton started the company in 1978.
