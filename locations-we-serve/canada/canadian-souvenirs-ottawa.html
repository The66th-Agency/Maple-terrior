In a small bottle, yes. The Canadian Air Transport Security Authority (CATSA) screens passengers at Canadian airports and treats maple syrup as a liquid and maple butter as a gel. In a carry-on, each container must be 100 mL or 100 g or less, and all of them must fit in one clear, resealable bag of no more than 1 L per passenger, which you take out at screening. Our 50 mL and 100 mL leaf bottles fit. A 250 mL bottle, the maple butter jar and the gift set go in your checked bag, which CATSA also suggests for any souvenir holding syrup. Shortbread, stroopwafels, nuts, chocolate and tea bags are solid food, allowed in both bags on a flight within Canada. Read on CATSA's pages on maple syrup, liquids and non-solid food and solid food on November 6, 2026; check them again before you fly.

Security in Ottawa follows the same rules for your flight out, but CATSA notes that once your destination is outside Canada, any food you carry, checked bags included, falls under the rules of the country you land in.
