Packing Maple for a Flight Out of Toronto

100mL

The most syrup one container can hold in your carry-on

The Canadian Air Transport Security Authority (CATSA) screens passengers at Toronto Pearson and Billy Bishop. CATSA applies its liquids rule to maple syrup and maple spreads, so in a carry-on each container must be 100 mL or smaller, and every container has to fit in one clear, resealable 1 L bag per passenger. A 250 mL bottle is over the limit, so it rides in the hold.

Carry-on

Cookies, stroopwafels, chocolate, tea

Shortbread, stroopwafels, chocolate maple almonds and cherries, peanuts, tea bags and maple sugar stay solid at room temperature. CATSA allows solid food in either bag and does not hold it to the 100 mL limit.

Checked bag

Syrup, maple butter, gift sets

Our 250 mL bottles, the 160 g jar of maple butter and any gift set with a bottle inside. Wrap each bottle in a sweater and pack it in the middle of the case, away from the shell.

Flying home to another country? Check that country's rules on bringing in food before you pack. Carry-on rules from the CATSA pages on maple syrup and on liquids and non-solid food, checked October 3, 2026. CATSA can change its rules, so check again before you fly.
