What Gives Quebec Maple Syrup Its Taste

Maple syrup is maple sap boiled down, with nothing added. Quebec producers made 90 percent of Canada's maple syrup in 2025, and our guide compares Quebec and Vermont maple syrup. You taste three things in every bottle: the forest, the thaw and the boil.

01

The forest

In Quebec, syrup comes from two kinds of tree, the sugar maple and the red maple, and the soil they grow in changes their sap: researchers at SUNY's College of Environmental Science and Forestry found sweeter sap on soils with more available nitrogen. Every bottle we sell comes from one forest, on one farm in Quebec's Appalachian Mountains.

02

The thaw

Sap runs when a night below freezing turns into a day above it. In the warmth the wood expands and pushes sap out through the taps, and in the cold it contracts and draws more up from the roots. Quebec's season runs from late February into early May, and a sugar bush makes its whole year of syrup in about 20 to 25 days.

03

The boil

It takes about 40 liters of sap to make one liter of syrup. As the sap boils down, its sugars react with its amino acids and brown, in the same Maillard reaction that browns a bread crust. The syrup gets its color and its maple flavor in that step.

Why Early Syrup Is Golden and Late Syrup Is Dark

Later in the season, in warmer weather, the sap holds less sugar and picks up more of the bacteria and yeast it meets once it leaves the tree. They break some of its sucrose into simpler sugars, which brown further in the boil, so the syrup comes out darker, with a stronger maple flavor.

How Canada grades it. The Canadian Food Inspection Agency sorts Canada Grade A maple syrup into these four color classes by how much light passes through it, from 75 percent or more for Golden to less than 25 percent for Very Dark. Read our guide to the four grades for what each one suits.

Why some late syrup stays light. Sugarmakers clean their equipment often and welcome a cold snap, because both slow the bacteria and yeast and keep syrup lighter for longer. Both our grades come from one farm, so what you taste between them is how early or late in the season the sap ran.

Questions About Golden and Dark Maple Syrup

Something else? Email info@mapleterroir.com and our family will answer.

Is dark maple syrup better than golden?

Neither is a better syrup. All four color classes are Canada Grade A, and Canada sets the same rules for each: the same density, no fermentation and no sediment. Choose by taste: Golden for a light, delicate maple flavor, and Dark when you want the maple to stand out.

Why is some maple syrup darker than others?

Syrup made from late-season sap usually comes out darker. In warmer weather the sap carries less sugar and more bacteria and yeast, which turn some of its sucrose into simpler sugars that brown in the boil. Producers who boil their sap longer also get darker syrup.

What do you use dark maple syrup for?

Use it in baking, coffee and glazes, where a lighter syrup fades behind the other ingredients, and on pancakes when you want to taste the maple through the butter. Our Dark syrup comes in the maple leaf bottle.

Is golden maple syrup the same as golden syrup?

No. Golden maple syrup is pure maple syrup in Canada's lightest color class. Golden syrup is a different product, a by-product of refining sugar cane into sugar. Our Golden syrup is pure maple syrup with nothing added.
