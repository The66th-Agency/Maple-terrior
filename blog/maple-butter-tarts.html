Maple Butter Tarts: The Canadian Classic Made With Pure Maple Syrup

Whisk pure maple syrup, brown sugar, melted butter, one egg and a little vinegar, spoon it into 12 unbaked tart shells and bake at 400°F (200°C) for 15 to 18 minutes, until the filling bubbles. Take them out at the shorter time for a runny center, or bake to the longer time for a set one.

A maple butter tart is a small pastry shell filled with butter, sugar and egg, with pure maple syrup in place of the corn syrup many butter tart recipes use. The filling bakes into a gooey caramel with a crackled top, and the maple gives it a deeper taste than corn syrup can.

The recipe card below makes a dozen in a standard muffin tin with store-bought or homemade shells. After it you will find how to get a runny or a firm center, how to stop the filling boiling over, which maple syrup to use, raisins against pecans, and how long the tarts keep.

Maple Butter Tarts

Prep15 min
Bake15 to 18 min
Total35 min
Makes12 tarts

Ingredients

  • 12unbaked tart shells, about 7.5 cm (3 inch), chilled
  • ½ cup (125 mL)pure maple syrup
  • ½ cup (100 g)packed brown sugar
  • ¼ cup (55 g)butter, melted and slightly cooled
  • 1large egg
  • 1 tspvanilla extract
  • 1 tspwhite vinegar or lemon juice
  • ¼ tspsalt
  • ⅓ cupraisins or chopped pecans, optional

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 400°F (200°C) with a rack in the lowest position. Set the tart shells in a muffin tin, or on a baking sheet if they come in foil cups, and keep them in the fridge until you fill them.
  2. Whisk the maple syrup, brown sugar and melted butter until the sugar has mostly dissolved. Whisk in the egg, vanilla, vinegar and salt until smooth, without beating in air.
  3. If you are using raisins or pecans, put a teaspoon or two in the bottom of each shell.
  4. Fill each shell two thirds full with the filling, about 1½ tablespoons each. A fuller shell boils over.
  5. Bake for 15 to 18 minutes, until the pastry is golden and the filling has puffed and bubbled. At 15 minutes the center is runny; at 18 it is set.
  6. Cool in the tin for 10 minutes, then run a knife around any tart that has stuck and lift them onto a rack. The filling firms as it cools.

Tools

  • 12-cup muffin tin
  • Mixing bowl and whisk
  • Tablespoon, to fill
  • Cooling rack

Runny or Firm: Choosing Your Center

Butter tart fans split into two camps, and the same filling serves both. The bake time and one optional addition decide which you get.

CenterHow to get itWhat you see when it is done
RunnyBake 15 minutes; serve the same dayEdges bubbling, the middle still loose when you nudge the tin
Soft-setBake 16 to 17 minutesThe middle wobbles slightly, like a soft jelly
FirmBake 18 minutes, or add 1 tablespoon of flour to the fillingThe top has crackled and the middle barely moves

Ovens vary, so watch the first batch and note the minute that gives you the center you like. The filling keeps firming for about half an hour as it cools, so take the tarts out a little runnier than you want to eat them.

What Each Ingredient Does

Pure maple syrup and brown sugar

Together they make the caramel. The maple syrup brings the flavor and keeps the filling soft; the brown sugar gives body and the crackled top. Use pure maple syrup, made only from boiled maple sap. Pancake or table syrup is mostly corn syrup with flavoring and gives a sweet tart with little maple in it; our guide to pure maple syrup vs table syrup shows how to read the label.

Butter and egg

The egg sets the filling as it bakes, and the butter makes it rich. Let the melted butter cool for a few minutes before the egg goes in, so the egg does not start to cook in the bowl.

Vinegar, vanilla and salt

A teaspoon of vinegar or lemon juice cuts the sweetness; you will not taste it on its own. Vanilla rounds out the maple, and salt keeps the filling from tasting flat.

Fixing the Common Problems

The filling boiled over

The shells were too full. Fill to two thirds; the filling rises as it bubbles and falls back as it cools. A baking sheet on the rack below catches any overflow.

The pastry bottoms are pale

Bake on the lowest rack, where the heat reaches the bottom of the tin first, and fill cold shells straight from the fridge.

The tarts stuck to the tin

Filling that bubbled over the edge has turned to caramel. Loosen each tart with a thin knife while it is still warm; once the caramel sets, they are much harder to free.

The filling is grainy

The brown sugar did not dissolve. Whisk it with the warm butter and syrup for a full minute before the egg goes in.

Which Maple Syrup to Use

A dark syrup keeps the most maple flavor through the oven, so it is our first choice for butter tarts. A golden syrup makes a lighter, sweeter tart. The grades differ in color and strength of taste, as our guide to maple syrup grades explains, and our guide to the best maple syrup for baking covers how each behaves in the oven.

Raisins, Pecans or Plain

The add-in is the other great butter tart argument. Put it in the shell before the filling, so it stays under the caramel instead of floating and burning on top.

  • Plain: all filling, the most maple in every bite.
  • Raisins: soak ⅓ cup in hot water for 10 minutes and pat them dry, so they stay plump and do not pull moisture from the filling.
  • Pecans or walnuts: ⅓ cup chopped and lightly toasted; the tarts then eat like small maple pecan pies.
  • Coconut or chocolate chips: 2 tablespoons per batch, for a sweeter tart.

For other maple sweets for the same table, our maple fudge and maple taffy on snow use the same syrup.

Storing and Freezing

Butter tarts have an egg filling, so we store them as Health Canada's food storage chart gives for egg dishes: 3 to 4 days in the fridge at 4°C (40°F) or colder, and 2 to 3 months in the freezer. Keep them in one layer in a covered container.

To freeze, set the cooled tarts on a tray until firm, then pack them in a freezer bag. Thaw them in the fridge overnight, and warm them for 5 minutes at 350°F (180°C) if you like the filling runny again.

Key Takeaways

  • For 12 tarts: ½ cup pure maple syrup, ½ cup brown sugar, ¼ cup melted butter, 1 egg, vanilla, vinegar and salt.
  • Fill cold shells two thirds full and bake at 400°F (200°C) on the lowest rack.
  • 15 minutes gives a runny center, 18 minutes a firm one.
  • Put raisins or pecans in the shell before the filling.
  • Keep them 3 to 4 days in the fridge or 2 to 3 months frozen, per Health Canada's times for egg dishes.
