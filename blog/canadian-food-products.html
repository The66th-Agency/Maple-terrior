Being Canadian means two different things: where a food is made, which the label tells you, and who owns the brand, which it does not. What the Labels Mean

Canada's food labels have rules for origin claims, set by the Canadian Food Inspection Agency. Making a claim is voluntary, but a claim a company makes should meet CFIA's guidelines. Here is what each one tells you, strongest first.

None of them tells you who owns the brand: the rules cover the food, its ingredients and its processing, and say nothing about ownership, which is why the list above has an Owned by line for every brand.

100% Canadian
The food, or the ingredient the claim names, is entirely Canadian.
Product of Canada, or Canadian
All or virtually all major ingredients, processing and labour are Canadian. Ingredients Canada does not generally produce, such as spices or cane sugar, may make up a small share, generally under 2 percent in total.
Made in Canada from domestic and imported ingredients
The last substantial transformation happened in Canada, for example baking the dough into cookies, with a mix of Canadian and imported ingredients, whatever the Canadian share.
Made in Canada from imported ingredients
Made in Canada from ingredients that all came from outside Canada. Every Made in Canada claim on food must carry a qualifying statement, such as one of these two.
Prepared for
Prepared for a retailer in Canada. This does not mean the food was prepared in Canada.

Source: the Canadian Food Inspection Agency's pages Origin claims on food labels and How to identify Canadian food, read November 19, 2026.

How to Choose Canadian Food

Start with what you want your money to do. If you want the food itself grown and made here, read the label. If you want the company to be Canadian, read the brand's own site, because the label will not say.

For the most Canadian content
Look for Product of Canada or 100% Canadian on the pack. Made in Canada alone can mean imported ingredients, so read the words after it.
For a Canadian-owned company
Pick a brand whose Owned by line above names a Canadian family, co-operative or company: Ganong, Dare, Chapman's, Gay Lea or Kozlik's among them.
To send Canadian food to someone
Choose foods that keep and travel: maple syrup, wrapped stroopwafels, popcorn, tea, chocolate or mustard. Maple Terroir's Canadian gift baskets put several of these in one box. The list of the best Canadian gifts goes beyond food.
For a team or a client list
Case prices cut the cost per person: a Maple Terroir stroopwafel caddy is $1.14 a stroopwafel and 44 bags of maple popcorn are $3.25 a bag. Made-in-Canada corporate gifts covers ordering for a team.

The claim "Canadian" means the same thing, and "100% Canadian" means entirely Canadian.
