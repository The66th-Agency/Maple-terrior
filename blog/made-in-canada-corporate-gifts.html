How to Check a Corporate Gift Is Made in Canada

"Product of Canada" and "Made in Canada" are different claims under Canadian rules, and a company based in Canada can sell gifts made anywhere. Here is what each label claim means.

Food and drink

Rules from the Canadian Food Inspection Agency

"Product of Canada"All or virtually all of the major ingredients, processing and labour are Canadian. Foreign material is negligible, generally under 2 percent.

Generally 98 percent or more Canadian

"Made in Canada from imported ingredients"The last substantial transformation happened in Canada, and the label must add a qualifying statement such as "from imported ingredients" or "from domestic and imported ingredients".

Everything else

Rules from the Competition Bureau

"Product of Canada"At least 98 percent of the product's content is Canadian, and the last substantial transformation happened in Canada.

98 percent Canadian content

"Made in Canada"At least 51 percent Canadian content, the last substantial transformation in Canada, and a qualifying statement that the product contains imported content.

51 percent Canadian content

Sources: Canadian Food Inspection Agency, origin claims on food labels and Competition Bureau, Product of Canada and Made in Canada claims, both read November 6, 2026.

Three Questions to Ask Before You Order

  1. What does the label say, word for word? "Product of Canada" is the stronger claim. "Made in Canada" with a qualifying statement means some content came from elsewhere. A Canadian brand name or address on the box describes the company, so read the origin claim itself.
  2. Where does the main ingredient or material come from? Cocoa, coffee and tea do not grow in Canada, so Canadian chocolate, coffee and tea are made or finished here from imported ingredients. That is normal, and the label should say so.
  3. Where was it made, apart from where the company is based? A gift company headquartered in Canada can fill its boxes with imported products. Ask the company what share of its products are made in Canada, as some publish it.

Corporate Gifts for Employees and for Clients

The same gift lands differently depending on who receives it, and a gift to employees has a tax rule attached.

Gifts for employees

  • The Canada Revenue Agency does not tax an employee on non-cash gifts and awards worth a combined $500 or less a year, taxes included.
  • Cash and near-cash gifts, such as most gift cards, are a taxable benefit at any amount.
  • Small items of trivial value, which the agency's examples name as coffee or tea, T-shirts and mugs, do not count toward the $500.
  • One gift for everyone keeps it fair and simple to order: a case of 48 maple leaf bottles covers 48 people at $5.99 each.

Source: Canada Revenue Agency, gifts, awards and long-service awards, read November 6, 2026. Ask your accountant about your own payroll.

Gifts for clients

  • Some companies limit the value of gifts their staff can accept, and some ask staff to share gifts with the office. Ask your contact, or choose something that suits a shared kitchen.
  • Food that can be shared, such as a box of stroopwafels or a gift set, reaches the whole team at a client's office.
  • A card in your own words matters more than the price. Name one thing the client did this year and what it meant for your business.
  • For a client who cannot accept gifts, a donation to a Canadian charity in their company's name says thank you without a parcel.
