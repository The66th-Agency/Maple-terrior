Maple Glazed Carrots: A One-Pan Recipe With Pure Maple Syrup

Simmer sliced carrots in a covered skillet with butter, maple syrup and a little water, then uncover and boil the liquid down until it coats them in a glossy glaze. One pan, about 25 minutes, serves four. An oven-roasted method is below.

By Maple Terroir 9 min read
Published November 6, 2026 Last updated November 6, 2026

Maple glazed carrots are carrots cooked until tender in butter and maple syrup, then finished over high heat until the syrup turns into a shiny glaze. The carrots' own sweetness and the maple meet in the pan, and a squeeze of lemon at the end keeps the side dish from tasting like dessert.

The stovetop method below is the fastest: the carrots steam under a lid, and the same liquid becomes the glaze once the lid comes off. The oven method takes a little longer and gives browned edges. Both use the same ingredients, so pick the one that suits the rest of your dinner. After the recipe card you will find how to cut the carrots, how to tell when the glaze is ready, which maple syrup to use, how to scale it for a holiday table, six flavor changes and how to store leftovers.

Maple Glazed Carrots

Prep10 min
Cook15 min
Total25 min
Serves4 as a side

Ingredients

  • 680 g (1½ lb)carrots, about 8 medium
  • ¾ cup (180 mL)water
  • 2 tbsp (30 g)butter
  • 3 tbsp (45 mL)pure maple syrup, divided
  • ½ tspkosher salt, or ¼ tsp fine salt
  • ¼ tspblack pepper
  • 1 tspfresh lemon juice
  • 1 tbspchopped parsley, or 1 tsp thyme leaves

Method

  1. Peel the carrots and cut them on a diagonal into slices 1 cm (½ inch) thick.
  2. Put the carrots, water, butter, 2 tablespoons of the maple syrup and the salt in a 30 cm (12 inch) skillet. Bring to a boil over medium-high heat.
  3. Cover, turn the heat to medium and simmer for 6 to 8 minutes, until a paring knife slides into a slice with a little resistance.
  4. Uncover, turn the heat back to medium-high and cook for 4 to 6 minutes, stirring often, until the liquid has boiled down to a glaze that coats the carrots and the pan is almost dry.
  5. Take the pan off the heat. Stir in the last tablespoon of maple syrup, the lemon juice and the pepper, scatter the herbs and serve.

Tools

  • 30 cm (12 inch) skillet with a lid
  • Vegetable peeler
  • Chef's knife
  • Paring knife, to test

What Each Ingredient Does

Each ingredient has one job. The water cooks the carrots and then disappears, the butter and maple syrup become the glaze, and the salt, pepper and lemon keep it savory. Knowing the jobs lets you change one without losing the dish.

Pure maple syrup, added twice

Two tablespoons go in at the start and cook down with the butter into the glaze. The last tablespoon goes in off the heat, so the finished carrots taste clearly of maple rather than only sweet. Use pure maple syrup, made only from boiled maple sap. Pancake or table syrup is mostly corn syrup with flavoring and turns thin and sugary in the pan; our guide to pure maple syrup vs table syrup explains how to read the label.

Water and butter

The water steams the carrots under the lid. When you uncover the pan it boils away, and the butter and syrup left behind thicken around the carrots. Too little water and the carrots stay hard before the pan dries; too much and you wait longer for the glaze. Three quarters of a cup suits a 30 cm pan.

Salt, pepper and lemon

Salt goes in with the water so it seasons the carrots as they cook. Pepper and lemon go in at the end. The lemon is a small amount, but it is what makes a sweet glaze taste balanced next to roast meat or fish.

How to Cut the Carrots

Cut every piece to the same thickness, so they all finish together. The thickness, not the shape, sets the cooking time. These are the four cuts that suit this recipe, with the covered simmering time for each on the stovetop.

Step by Step: Getting the Glaze Right

The method has two stages in one pan: cook the carrots under a lid, then boil off the liquid with the lid off. The details below decide whether you end up with a glaze or a puddle.

Cook them covered until almost done

Keep the lid on and the liquid at a steady simmer. Test at 6 minutes. You want the knife to meet a little resistance in the center, because the carrots keep cooking while the glaze reduces. Fully soft at this stage means mushy at the table.

Reduce the liquid with the lid off

Turn the heat up and stir every 30 seconds or so. The bubbles start large and watery, then turn smaller, slower and shiny. The glaze is ready when a spoon drawn across the pan leaves a clear trail that fills back in slowly, and the carrots look lacquered rather than wet.

Stop before it browns

Maple syrup is mostly sugar, and once the water has gone it browns fast. A little color at the edges of the pan is fine; dark spots mean it is time to take the pan off the heat. If the glaze catches, add a tablespoon of water and stir to loosen it.

Oven-Roasted Maple Glazed Carrots

Roasting gives browned, slightly chewy edges and frees the stovetop, which helps when the oven is already on for a roast. It takes 25 to 30 minutes at 425°F (220°C).

  1. Heat the oven to 425°F (220°C) and line a baking tray with parchment, because maple syrup scorches onto a bare tray.
  2. Toss 680 g (1½ lb) of carrots, cut as for the stovetop or halved lengthwise if slim, with 1 tablespoon of oil and ½ teaspoon of kosher salt. Spread them in one layer with space between.
  3. Roast for 15 minutes. Meanwhile, stir together 2 tablespoons of maple syrup and 1 tablespoon of melted butter.
  4. Pour the maple butter over the carrots, toss and roast for another 10 to 15 minutes, until tender and caramelized at the edges.
  5. Finish with the last tablespoon of maple syrup, the lemon, pepper and herbs, as in the recipe card.

The syrup goes on halfway, not at the start, so it browns without burning. In an air fryer, cook the oiled carrots at 400°F (200°C) for 10 minutes, shaking once, then add the maple butter and cook 3 to 5 minutes more.

Which Maple Syrup to Use

A dark, robust syrup gives the strongest maple taste after it has been cooked down with butter, so it is our first choice for glazed carrots. A golden syrup works too and gives a lighter, more delicate glaze. The grades differ in color and strength of flavor, as our guide to maple syrup grades explains.

Making Them for a Crowd

Scale the carrots, syrup and butter together, and add pans rather than piling one pan higher. A crowded skillet steams instead of glazing, so past four servings use two skillets or roast on two trays.

For a holiday dinner, do the covered simmer up to a day ahead: cook the carrots until just tender, drain off any liquid into a container, and chill both. Twenty minutes before dinner, put the carrots and liquid back in the skillet with the butter and syrup and boil it down to a glaze, about 6 to 8 minutes.

Six Ways to Change the Glaze

Keep the carrots, butter and maple syrup, and change one other thing. Each of these goes in with the last tablespoon of syrup unless it says otherwise.

  • Orange: the zest of one orange and 2 tablespoons of its juice in place of the lemon.
  • Dijon: 1 teaspoon of Dijon mustard, for a sharper, savory glaze that suits pork.
  • Ginger: 1 teaspoon of grated fresh ginger, added with the water at the start.
  • Chili: a pinch of chili flakes or ¼ teaspoon of cayenne for a little heat.
  • Brown butter and pecans: brown the butter in the empty skillet first, then add the rest; finish with ¼ cup of toasted chopped pecans.
  • Balsamic: 1 teaspoon of balsamic vinegar in place of the lemon, for a darker, tangier glaze.

What to Serve Them With

Glazed carrots go with anything roasted or grilled, and they suit a Thanksgiving or Christmas dinner because they cook on the stovetop while the oven is full.

  • Fish: our maple glazed salmon bakes in 12 to 15 minutes, about the time these take on the stovetop, for a full maple dinner.
  • Roast turkey or chicken, where the lemon in the glaze cuts the richness of the gravy.
  • Pork chops or ham, with the Dijon version of the glaze; our maple glazed ham uses the same syrup and mustard, so the two share a bottle.
  • A vegetarian plate with roasted squash and a grain such as farro, using oil or a plant butter in the glaze.

Storing and Reheating

Cooked vegetable dishes keep 3 to 4 days in the fridge and 2 to 3 months in the freezer, according to Health Canada's food storage chart. Store the carrots in a sealed container in a fridge at 4°C (40°F) or colder.

Reheat them in a skillet over medium heat with a tablespoon of water, stirring until the glaze loosens and turns glossy again, about 3 minutes. The microwave works too, covered, though the glaze stays softer. Frozen carrots come out softer than fresh ones, so they suit soups and mash better than the holiday table.

Key Takeaways

  • For four servings: 680 g carrots, ¾ cup water, 2 tablespoons butter and 3 tablespoons pure maple syrup in a 30 cm skillet.
  • Simmer covered 6 to 8 minutes, then boil uncovered 4 to 6 minutes until the liquid becomes a glaze.
  • Add the last tablespoon of maple syrup and the lemon off the heat.
  • Roasting takes 25 to 30 minutes at 425°F (220°C), with the syrup added halfway.
  • Leftovers keep 3 to 4 days in the fridge, per Health Canada.
