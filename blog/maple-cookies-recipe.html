Maple Cookies Recipe With Pure Maple Syrup and Maple Sugar

Beat butter with maple sugar, add an egg and ½ cup of pure maple syrup, then mix in the flour. Chill the dough for 1 hour, roll each ball in more maple sugar and bake at 350°F (180°C) for 10 to 12 minutes, until the edges set and the centers are still soft. The recipe makes 19 cookies.

By Maple Terroir Recipe by Michelle Lopez, Hummingbird High 8 min read
Published November 18, 2026 Last updated November 18, 2026

An illustration of the finished cookies, made for this page. The recipe is Michelle Lopez's, and where it comes from is set out under the recipe card.

Maple cookies are cookies sweetened and flavored with maple. This maple cookies recipe makes drop cookies with two maple ingredients: pure maple syrup in the dough, and maple sugar in the dough and on the outside. Some maple cookie recipes use brown sugar and add maple extract. This one gets its maple from the syrup and the sugar, and uses no extract.

The recipe is the baker Michelle Lopez's, given as she published it, and each section below names the source behind its advice.

Maple Cookies

1 hrChill
10 to 12 minBake, each pan
About 2 hrTotal
19 cookiesMakes

Dough

  • 2¾ cups (350 g)all-purpose flour
  • ½ tspbaking powder
  • 1 tspkosher salt, or ½ tsp table salt
  • 1 cup (227 g)unsalted butter, at room temperature
  • 1 cup (198 g)maple sugar, or the same amount of brown sugar
  • 1large egg, at room temperature
  • ½ cup (156 g)pure maple syrup, at room temperature. The Dark maple syrup in our maple leaf bottle suits baking

Coating

  • ⅓ cup (67 g)maple sugar

Method

  1. Stir the flour, baking powder and salt together with a whisk in a medium bowl.
  2. Put the butter and the 1 cup of maple sugar in a stand mixer fitted with the paddle and beat on medium-high for 2 to 3 minutes, scraping the bowl as needed. The mixture should turn pale and fluffy and roughly double in size.
  3. Drop the speed to low and add the egg, then pour in the maple syrup in a slow stream. Stop mixing as soon as the syrup is worked in, and scrape the bowl.
  4. Still on low, add the flour mixture a bit at a time and stop when it is just worked in. Scrape the bowl once more and give the dough a final 30 seconds on low.
  5. Cover the bowl and chill the dough in the fridge for at least 1 hour. It can wait there for up to 3 days.
  6. Set a rack in the middle of the oven and heat it to 350°F (180°C). Cover two half sheet pans with parchment.
  7. Tip the ⅓ cup of maple sugar into a shallow bowl. Portion the dough with a 3-tablespoon scoop, roll each ball in the sugar until it is covered all over, and space the balls at least 3 inches (7.5 cm) apart on the pans.
  8. Bake a single pan for 10 to 12 minutes. The cookies are ready when the edges are set and the middles still look soft.
  9. Move the pan to a wire rack and leave the cookies on it for 20 minutes, until their edges and undersides are firm. They leave the oven puffed, then sink and crack as they cool.
  10. Bake the rest of the dough the same way.

Tools

  • Stand mixer with paddle
  • 3-tablespoon cookie scoop
  • Two half sheet pans and parchment
  • Wire rack

Where this recipe comes from. The quantities, temperature and times are from Soft and Chewy Maple Sugar Cookies by Michelle Lopez of Hummingbird High, the author of the cookbook Weeknight Baking. We set out her recipe in our own words and added Celsius and centimeters and her own substitution notes. Do not taste the raw dough: Health Canada's advice on flour is that even a small amount of raw dough could make you sick, and to wash your hands, bowls, utensils and work surfaces with soap after handling it.

One Box of Maple Sugar or Three

The recipe's author gives two ways to make these, and in our 100 g boxes of maple sugar they come to one box or three. She calls maple sugar a little on the pricey side, so she suggests brown sugar in the dough when you want a small amount to go further, with the maple sugar saved for the coating.

One box: maple sugar on the outside

Use brown sugar, light or dark, in place of the maple sugar in the dough and roll the dough balls in ⅓ cup (67 g) of maple sugar. The maple in the dough comes from the syrup, and every cookie is coated in maple sugar. One box covers the coating with some left for coffee.

Three boxes: maple sugar all the way through

Use maple sugar for both the dough and the coating, as the recipe is written: 1⅓ cups in all, 265 g by the author's weights. No brown or white sugar goes in, so all of the sweetness is maple.

The 198 g is the weight the recipe's author prints for 1 cup of maple sugar. A cup can weigh less than that, and one published baking weight chart lists 156 g, so without a scale follow her cup measure. Either way the all-maple version takes more than two 100 g boxes.

Our maple sugar is made in Canada from organic maple syrup, with nothing added, and comes in a 100 g box. Out of maple sugar altogether? The author's note is that white sugar works for the coating.

What the Maple Syrup and Maple Sugar Each Do

The syrup is the liquid maple and the sugar is the dry maple, and the recipe as written uses one of each. Made that way, a little over a third of the dough is maple by weight: 198 g of maple sugar and 156 g of syrup in about 981 g of dough.

Pure maple syrup

The syrup carries maple flavor into the dough, and it is also an extra liquid. Sally McKenney of Sally's Baking gives that as the reason she chills her own maple dough, which uses ⅓ cup of syrup: without chilling, she writes, her cookies spread into a greasy puddle. She also writes that the syrup helps give her cookies slightly crisp edges. Use pure maple syrup, made only from maple sap. Pancake or table syrup is mostly corn syrup with flavoring; our guide to pure maple syrup vs table syrup shows how to tell them apart on the label.

Maple sugar

Maple sugar is maple syrup boiled further and stirred until it dries into granules, so it adds maple without adding liquid. In this recipe it is beaten with the butter, as white sugar would be, and in other recipes it replaces white sugar cup for cup. Michelle Lopez credits the maple sugar for the texture of her cookies, which she describes as pillowy and chewy, and she writes not to skip the coating: without it, the cookies turn out puffy and cakey-looking.

How Long the Recipe Takes

Plan on about two hours for the first pan of cookies. About 20 minutes of that is mixing and shaping, which is our estimate and not a figure from the recipe. The chilling, baking and cooling times are the recipe's.

Mix and shapeAbout 20 minutes

Our estimate for making the dough and, after the chill, scooping and coating it.

Chill1 hour, up to 3 days

After more than 3 hours in the fridge, Sally McKenney lets her own maple dough sit on the counter for about 30 minutes before shaping.

Bake10 to 12 minutes a pan

To save time, the recipe's author says you can bake two pans at once: racks in the upper and lower thirds of the oven, pans swapped halfway through.

Cool20 minutes

On the pan. Every further pan adds its own bake and its own 20 minutes.

Which Maple Syrup to Bake With

Bake these with a Dark syrup. It has the strongest maple flavor of the grades we sell, so more of it is left after 12 minutes in the oven. A Golden syrup has a delicate taste that fades when it is baked, which makes it the one to pour over pancakes instead. Our guide to the best maple syrup for baking explains why, and the guide to maple syrup grades covers the whole color scale.

Sally McKenney notes that the darker syrup, made later in the season, has more flavor, though she says either grade works in her cookies. Have the syrup at room temperature before it goes into the mixer, as the recipe asks.

The Maple Glaze

The glaze is optional and takes two ingredients. Mix ⅔ cup of icing sugar (powdered sugar) with 3 tablespoons of maple syrup and drizzle it over cookies that have cooled all the way. Give it at least 1 hour at room temperature to set before you stack the cookies.

The quantities and the setting time are from Simple Bites' Soft Maple Sugar Cookies. The glaze is not cooked. Our baking guide builds a maple glaze on the Dark grade so the maple comes through clearly, and the same bottle covers the dough.

Variations and Swaps

Keep the quantities as they are and change one thing at a time. The first three are the recipe author's own; the rest name where they come from.

  • Maple pecan cookiesAdd 1 cup of nuts. Pecans are her pick, and she says any kind of nut works.
  • Maple chocolate chip cookiesMix in 1 cup (170 g) of chocolate chips.
  • Butter and saltSalted butter works in place of unsalted, and she found no noticeable difference in taste. With table salt in place of kosher salt, use half as much.
  • Maple extractNot needed here. Sally McKenney adds 1 teaspoon to her brown sugar dough because, in her tests, syrup alone did not give enough maple flavor.
  • Maple sandwich cookiesAn idea of ours, not part of the author's recipe: spread maple butter between two cooled cookies. Maple butter is pure maple syrup boiled and whipped into a spread, and you can also make maple butter at home.
  • Cut-out maple cookiesThis dough is made to be scooped. For shapes, use a dough made for rolling: the Maple Holiday Cookies recipe from Maple from Canada uses the same two maple ingredients, rolled ¼ inch (6 mm) thick and baked for 8 to 10 minutes at 350°F (180°C).

For more maple baking, our maple butter tarts use ½ cup of the same syrup, and maple fudge takes a spoonful of maple sugar on top.

The maple in this recipe

Maple sugar for the dough and the coating, Dark maple syrup for the dough and the glaze, and maple butter for the sandwich cookies in the variations above.

Fixing the Common Problems

The fixes below come from Michelle Lopez's and Sally McKenney's recipe notes, and the last one from our baking guide. Most of them are about chilling the dough, coating it and taking the cookies out on time.

What you seeLikely causeWhat to do
Flat, greasy cookiesThe dough was not chilled long enough, by Sally McKenney's note on her own maple doughChill the dough for the full hour. She also starts with butter that is at room temperature and still cool to the touch, not soft and greasy.
Puffy, cake-like cookiesThe dough balls were baked without the sugar coatingRoll every ball in maple sugar, or in white sugar if you have run out.
Soft centers at 12 minutesThat is how they should look when they come outTake them out once the edges are set, then leave them on the pan for 20 minutes, until the edges and bottoms feel firm.
Burnt bottoms or raw centersTwo pans baked at onceBake one pan at a time on the center rack, which is how the recipe's author gets even cookies.
Not much maple flavorA Golden syrup, or pancake syrup, in the doughUse a Dark pure maple syrup, and maple sugar in the dough as well as on the outside.

Storing and Freezing

The room temperature and fridge times are the recipe author's. The freezing time is Sally McKenney's, written for her own maple cookie dough, another chilled butter and maple syrup dough. She bakes her dough balls from frozen for an extra minute. Hers are half the size of these, so go by the edges: the cookies are done when the edges have set.

If you glaze the cookies, let the glaze set first and put parchment between the layers.

How long they keep

  • 3 daysbaked cookies, in an airtight container or a zip-top bag, at room temperature
  • 3 daysdough, covered, in the fridge
  • 3 monthsscooped dough balls or baked cookies, glazed or not, in the freezer
