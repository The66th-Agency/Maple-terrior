Best Canadian Gifts and Souvenirs to Bring Home

The best souvenirs from Canada, ranked by what visitors use after they get home. From maple syrup to Hudson's Bay blankets, here are the Canadian gifts worth bringing back.

By Maple Terroir May 19, 2026 8 min read

Visitors to Canada often ask what to take home. Here is our list of gifts that travel well and get used after the trip.

A good Canadian souvenir does three things: it comes from Canada, it gets used after the trip ends, and it tells the recipient something specific about the country.

The list below ranks 15 Canadian gifts and souvenirs, weighted toward items people keep using once they get home. Maple syrup leads the list. We sell it, so read the pick with that in mind: it travels well, keeps for years in a sealed bottle, and gets used in the kitchen long after the trip.

15 Best Canadian Gifts and Souvenirs, Ranked

01

Real Canadian Maple Syrup (Single-Origin Quebec)

The Top Pick

The version worth packing is pure maple syrup, and single-origin syrup from one Quebec farm tells the recipient exactly where it came from.

We recommend our own syrup here, since we publish this guide. Any single-origin syrup from Quebec makes a good gift, and if you want organic syrup, the three certifications to look for on the label are Ecocert, Canada Organic and USDA Organic. Our organic syrups hold all three.

Where to buy: online at mapleterroir.com. Maple Terroir is also carried by Costco, Save-On-Foods, Urban Fare and Shoppers Drug Mart. The Vancouver tourist guide is here if that is your city.

02

Niagara Ice Wine

Ice wine is made from grapes harvested frozen on the vine at minus eight Celsius or colder, which concentrates the sugar. Niagara, in Ontario, is one of the regions that makes it. Visitors flying home from Toronto can pick up bottles at the LCBO. Half-bottles travel well in checked luggage.

03

Hudson's Bay Striped Blanket

The Hudson's Bay point blanket dates to 1779, and its green, red, yellow and indigo stripes are known around the world as Canadian. The Hudson's Bay stores closed in June 2025, and Canadian Tire bought the brand and its stripes the same year. A real one is heavy, expensive, and lasts decades.

04

Indigenous Art (Inuit and Coast Salish)

Buying directly from Indigenous artists or accredited Indigenous-owned galleries supports the communities the art originates from and avoids the wholesale imitations sold in tourist shops. Inuit soapstone carvings, Coast Salish prints, Haida silver jewelry, and Métis beadwork all qualify. The Inuit Art Foundation certifies authentic Inuit work with a labeled disc. Look for that mark before buying anything sold as "Inuit-style."

05

Maple-Infused Chocolate and Maple Sugar

Maple syrup boiled until it crystallizes becomes maple sugar, which keeps for years without refrigeration and travels lighter than the syrup itself. We sell maple sugar, chocolate-covered maple almonds and Cocoa Chocolate Maple Stroopwafels, and other maple producers make their own versions.

06

Royal Canadian Mint Maple Leaf Coins

The Royal Canadian Mint produces the Gold Maple Leaf and Silver Maple Leaf coins, both legal tender and bullion-grade. The one-ounce silver coin is the gift of choice when the recipient values something they can either keep on a shelf or sell at any precious-metals dealer worldwide. Sold direct at the Mint's Ottawa boutique and online.

07

Canadian Whisky (Crown Royal, Canadian Club)

Crown Royal's purple-bag bottle reads as instantly Canadian and pairs well with the maple syrup theme for a layered gift box. Crown Royal and Canadian Club are both widely available at LCBO, BC Liquor and SAQ locations.

08

Pacific Smoked Salmon

The West Coast First Nations have been smoking salmon for thousands of years, and the product travels well sealed in vacuum-packed portions. Look for cold-smoked sockeye from BC. Visitors flying out of Vancouver can pick up smoked salmon at Granville Island Public Market or at YVR Duty Free.

09

Tim Hortons Coffee and Merch

Tim Hortons is Canada's largest coffee chain and a cultural reference most Canadians grew up with. The original blend ground coffee, the take-home pack of Smile cookies, and the merchandise (mugs, toques, hoodies) all read as Canadian gifts at a price point well under the premium options on this list. Available at any Tim Hortons across Canada and through the brand's online store.

10

Canadian Chocolate Bars

Canadians grow up on chocolate bars such as Coffee Crisp and Caramilk, and a multipack from any grocery store makes an inexpensive gift for someone who has never tried them.

11

Hockey Jersey from a Canadian Team

An authentic NHL jersey from the Montreal Canadiens, Toronto Maple Leafs, Vancouver Canucks, Calgary Flames, Edmonton Oilers, Ottawa Senators, or Winnipeg Jets is the strongest hockey gift available in Canada. Replica jerseys cost less than authentics and read identically at first glance. Buy direct from the team store or NHL official retailers.

12

Toques, Mittens, and Wool Socks Made in Canada

Look for wool toques, mittens and socks with a Made in Canada label, such as Stanfield's. The toque (the Canadian word for a beanie) is the most distinctly Canadian piece of clothing a visitor can take home.

13

Saskatoon Berry Preserves and Birch Syrup

Saskatoon berries grow wild across the Prairies and have no good US equivalent. Birch syrup, produced in Alaska and the Yukon, is much rarer than maple syrup and reads as adventurous in the same gift category. Both are shelf-stable and travel well in checked luggage. Available at farmers' markets across the Prairies and in specialty Canadian food shops.

14

Inukshuk Replica (Real, Not Tourist-Trap)

The inukshuk is a stone figure built by Inuit communities as a navigational marker, and it became the emblem of the 2010 Vancouver Olympics. Real inukshuks are best supported by buying small carved replicas from Inuit artists rather than mass-produced versions printed on mugs and keychains. Buy directly from Inuit artists, from Inuit-owned co-operatives, or from galleries whose Inuit work carries the Inuit Art Foundation's tag.

15

Canadian Outdoor Gear (Arc'teryx, Roots, Canada Goose)

Arc'teryx is headquartered in North Vancouver, Roots is a Canadian heritage brand, and Canada Goose makes parkas in Canada.

Why We Put Maple Syrup First

The Hudson's Bay blanket stays in the closet most of the year. The ice wine bottle gets opened once. The hockey jersey gets worn during games. The maple syrup gets poured on Sunday pancakes and on weeknight yogurt or oatmeal.

That is why we put it first. Over the life of a bottle, the recipient might pour it into coffee, over French toast, into salad dressing, on roasted carrots, and into a winter Old Fashioned.

When you choose a bottle, look for one that says where the syrup comes from. Ecocert, Canada Organic and USDA Organic are three separate organic certifications. Our organic syrups hold all three certifications, and our syrup comes from one family farm in Quebec's Appalachian Mountains.

The other practical consideration is getting it home. We ship to every Canadian province from Vancouver. Visitors who live in another province can order online and have it shipped home: Standard shipping takes 3 to 9 business days and Express 1 to 2, and orders of $99 CAD or more ship free. Visitors flying to another country should pack a 250 mL bottle in checked luggage, because airport security limits syrup in a carry-on to containers of 100 mL or less.

Best Canadian Gifts FAQ

We think pure Canadian maple syrup is the best souvenir, because people use it at home long after the trip. We sell it, so weigh our pick accordingly. The version worth buying is single-origin from one Quebec farm. Other strong choices include ice wine from Niagara, Hudson's Bay blankets, and Indigenous art from Coast Salish or Inuit artists.

The most distinctly Canadian gifts are maple syrup, ice wine, Hudson's Bay striped blankets, hockey jerseys, Tim Hortons coffee, maple-infused chocolates, Canadian whisky, smoked Pacific salmon, Inuit and Coast Salish art, Royal Canadian Mint maple leaf coins, toques, and Canadian-made wool mittens. The strongest gifts pair a national symbol with something the recipient can actually use.

Maple syrup is produced almost entirely in Canada and the northern United States, with Quebec accounting for most of the world supply. Other Canadian foods that travel well include Niagara ice wine, butter tarts, Saskatoon berry preserves, Pacific smoked salmon, and Canadian chocolate bars such as Coffee Crisp and Caramilk.

Maple syrup is allowed in checked luggage with no quantity limit. For carry-on, the 100 mL liquid rule applies, so most full-size bottles must travel in checked baggage. Visitors who live in another Canadian province can have it shipped home instead. We ship to every province, but not outside Canada.

For maple products, look for a bottle that says where the syrup comes from. Our single-origin Quebec maple syrup is sold online at mapleterroir.com and is also carried by Costco, Save-On-Foods, Urban Fare and Shoppers Drug Mart. For non-food gifts, buy from Indigenous artisan co-ops and from the Royal Canadian Mint, which sells online.
