The Best Canadian Gifts for 2026, Ranked

Fifteen Canadian gifts, ranked by what people keep using once the wrapping is off. Give them at home for the holidays, send them to family abroad, or pack them for the flight back.

By Maple Terroir 8 min read
Published May 19, 2026 Last updated October 19, 2026

A Canadian gift has two jobs: it has to say Canada, and it has to get used. This list covers holiday gifts, gifts to send abroad and souvenirs for visitors heading home.

A good Canadian souvenir does three things: it comes from Canada, it gets used after the trip ends, and it tells the recipient something specific about the country.

The list below ranks 15 Canadian gifts and souvenirs, weighted toward items people keep using once they get home. Maple syrup leads the list. We sell it, so read the pick with that in mind: it travels well, keeps for years in a sealed bottle, and gets used in the kitchen long after the trip.

15 Best Canadian Gifts and Souvenirs, Ranked

01

Pure Canadian Maple Syrup

Best for: anyone who cooks, and anyone abroad who misses Canada

Canada makes about 71 percent of the world's maple syrup, according to Maple from Canada, and a bottle gets used long after the holidays: on pancakes, in coffee, in salad dressing. Look for a label that names where the syrup comes from. For organic syrup, the certifications to look for are Ecocert, Canada Organic and USDA Organic.

We sell maple syrup, so read this pick with that in mind. Plenty of Canadian producers make excellent syrup, and our ranking of Canadian maple syrup brands compares them side by side.

Where to buy: grocery stores across Canada, sugar shacks in eastern Canada, or our online store.

02

Niagara Ice Wine

Best for: a host, or a dessert-wine fan

Ice wine is made from grapes picked frozen on the vine at minus eight Celsius or colder, which concentrates the sugar. Niagara, in Ontario, is the best-known region for it. Half-bottles of 200 or 375 mL are the usual gift size and travel well in checked luggage.

Where to buy: LCBO in Ontario, or at the wineries along the Niagara wine route.

03

Hudson's Bay Stripes Point Blanket

Best for: a once-in-a-lifetime gift

The Hudson's Bay point blanket dates to 1779, and its green, red, yellow and indigo stripes are known around the world as Canadian. The Hudson's Bay stores closed in June 2025, and Canadian Tire bought the brand and its stripes the same year. A real one is heavy, expensive and lasts decades.

Where to buy: Canadian Tire's Hudson's Bay Stripes collection, which carries the point blanket in several sizes.

04

Inuit and Coast Salish Art

Best for: a gift that supports the artist directly

Inuit soapstone carvings and prints, Coast Salish prints, Haida silver jewelry and Métis beadwork are works of art, and buying from the artist or an Indigenous-owned gallery keeps the money with the community. For Inuit work, look for the Igloo Tag, a label the federal government created in 1958 and the Inuit Art Foundation has run since 2017. It names the artist, the community and the year the work was made.

Where to buy: Dorset Fine Arts, the marketing arm of the Kinngait co-operative, or a licensed gallery. The Inuit Art Foundation's Igloo Tag page explains what the label means.

05

Maple Sugar, Maple Candy and Maple Chocolate

Best for: a stocking or a care package, since nothing can spill

Maple syrup boiled until it crystallizes becomes maple sugar, which keeps without refrigeration and weighs less than the syrup. Maple candy and maple chocolate make the same idea easier to share. Every sugar shack and most Canadian maple producers make their own.

Where to buy: sugar shacks, farmers' markets and grocery stores. We sell maple sugar, maple stroopwafels and a shelf of Canadian stocking stuffers from $4.99.

06

Royal Canadian Mint Maple Leaf Coins

Best for: a keepsake that holds its value

The Royal Canadian Mint makes the Gold Maple Leaf and Silver Maple Leaf, both legal tender and bullion coins. The one-ounce silver coin sits on a shelf as a keepsake and can be sold to a precious-metals dealer later. The Mint also makes collector coins for each year.

Where to buy: the Royal Canadian Mint online store, or its boutiques in Ottawa and Winnipeg.

07

Canadian Whisky

Best for: a whisky drinker, or a gift box with maple syrup

Crown Royal comes in its purple bag and reads as Canadian at a glance; Canadian Club is the older name. Both are Canadian rye-style whiskies sold in every province.

Where to buy: LCBO, BC Liquor Stores or the SAQ.

08

Pacific Smoked Salmon

Best for: a food lover, packed in checked luggage

West Coast First Nations have smoked salmon for thousands of years. Look for wild sockeye from British Columbia, either hot-smoked in a shelf-stable gift box or vacuum-packed and kept cold.

Where to buy: the Granville Island Public Market in Vancouver, or fishmongers along the BC coast.

09

Tim Hortons Coffee and Mugs

Best for: a Canadian living abroad, under $25

Tim Hortons is one of the best-known coffee chains in Canada and part of growing up here for many Canadians. A bag of Original Blend and a mug cost little and mean a lot to someone far from home.

Where to buy: any Tim Hortons, most grocery stores, or timhortons.ca.

10

Canadian Chocolate Bars

Best for: friends abroad, under $15

Coffee Crisp and Caramilk are two bars Canadians grow up on and visitors rarely find at home. A mixed bag from any grocery store makes an easy add-on to a bigger gift.

Where to buy: any grocery or drug store. Nestlé Canada lists the Coffee Crisp range.

11

A Jersey from a Canadian NHL Team

Best for: a hockey fan

Seven NHL teams play in Canada: Montreal, Toronto, Ottawa, Winnipeg, Calgary, Edmonton and Vancouver. A replica jersey costs less than an authentic one and looks the same from a few steps away.

Where to buy: each team's arena store, or the NHL Shop.

12

Toques, Mittens and Wool Socks Made in Canada

Best for: someone heading into their first Canadian winter

The toque, the Canadian word for a knit winter hat, is the most Canadian piece of clothing there is. Look for a Made in Canada label: Stanfield's has made wool clothing in Truro, Nova Scotia, since the 1800s.

Where to buy: Stanfield's, Roots or local craft markets.

13

Saskatoon Berry Jam and Birch Syrup

Best for: someone who has already tried maple syrup

Saskatoon berries grow wild across the Prairies and taste a little like a blueberry with almond notes. Birch syrup is made in small batches across Canada, as far north as the Yukon, and tastes sharper and more savory than maple. Both jars keep on a shelf.

Where to buy: farmers' markets on the Prairies, and specialty food shops that stock local producers.

14

An Inuksuk Carved by an Inuit Artist

Best for: a gift with a story

An inuksuk is a stone figure Inuit built as a marker on the land, and one became the emblem of the 2010 Vancouver Winter Olympics. A small carved inuksuk by an Inuit artist is worth far more than the printed versions on mugs and keychains.

Where to buy: Inuit-owned co-operatives and galleries that sell Igloo Tag work, such as Dorset Fine Arts.

15

Canadian Outdoor Gear

Best for: a splurge

Arc'teryx is based in North Vancouver, Roots is a Toronto heritage brand known for sweats and leather, and Canada Goose makes its parkas in Canada. Each is a gift the recipient wears for years.

Where to buy: Arc'teryx, Roots and Canada Goose.

How We Ranked Them

Each pick had to come from Canada and get used after the wrapping comes off. Gifts used every week ranked above gifts kept on a shelf, and gifts that travel well ranked above fragile ones. Flying with a gift? Airport security in Canada limits liquids such as syrup, jam and wine in a carry-on to containers of 100 mL or less, so larger bottles go in checked luggage.

Best Canadian Gifts FAQ

We think pure Canadian maple syrup is the best souvenir, because people use it at home long after the trip. We sell it, so weigh our pick accordingly. The version worth buying is single-origin from one farm. Other strong choices include ice wine from Niagara, Hudson's Bay blankets, and Indigenous art from Coast Salish or Inuit artists.

The most distinctly Canadian gifts are maple syrup, ice wine, Hudson's Bay striped blankets, hockey jerseys, Tim Hortons coffee, maple-infused chocolates, Canadian whisky, smoked Pacific salmon, Inuit and Coast Salish art, Royal Canadian Mint maple leaf coins, toques, and Canadian-made wool mittens. The strongest gifts pair a national symbol with something the recipient can actually use.

Maple syrup is produced almost entirely in Canada and the northern United States, with Quebec accounting for most of the world supply. Other Canadian foods that travel well include Niagara ice wine, butter tarts, Saskatoon berry preserves, Pacific smoked salmon, and Canadian chocolate bars such as Coffee Crisp and Caramilk.

Maple syrup is allowed in checked luggage with no quantity limit. For carry-on, the 100 mL liquid rule applies, so most full-size bottles must travel in checked baggage. Visitors who live in another Canadian province can have it shipped home instead. We ship to every province, but not outside Canada.

For maple products, look for a bottle that says where the syrup comes from. To send a boxed maple gift set to someone in another city, our gift basket pages for Edmonton, Ottawa and Toronto compare the boxes by price and occasion. For non-food gifts, buy from Indigenous artisan co-ops and from the Royal Canadian Mint, which sells online.
