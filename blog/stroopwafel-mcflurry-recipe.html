Stroopwafel McFlurry Recipe: Make It at Home With Maple Stroopwafels

Beat 2 cups of vanilla ice cream in a cold bowl until it turns thick like soft serve, then fold in two chopped maple stroopwafels and a quick maple caramel. No machine, about 30 minutes, and it serves two.

By Maple Terroir 8 min read
Published November 6, 2026 Last updated November 6, 2026

A stroopwafel McFlurry is vanilla ice cream beaten soft, with chopped stroopwafel and caramel folded through it. McDonald's Canada's menu page lists one as vanilla soft serve with caramel-flavored cinnamon waffle cone pieces and caramel sauce, and tells you to enjoy it while it is here (checked November 6, 2026). So it comes and goes.

This version is yours any day of the year. It uses real stroopwafels, the thin Dutch waffle cookies held together by a syrup filling, and a three-ingredient caramel made from pure maple syrup. Below is the recipe card, then how to get the soft-serve texture without a machine, why the cookie matters, how it compares with the drive-thru cup, five flavor changes, fixes for common problems and answers to the questions people ask.

Homemade Stroopwafel McFlurry

Prep10 min
Cook5 min
Total30 min
Serves2

Ingredients

For the maple caramel

  • ¼ cup (60 mL)pure maple syrup
  • 1 tbsp (15 g)butter
  • 3 tbsp (45 mL)whipping cream (35%)
  • 1 pinchsalt

For the McFlurry

  • 2 cups (500 mL)vanilla ice cream, about 4 scoops
  • 3maple syrup stroopwafels, 34 g each
  • 1 to 2 tbspcold milk, only if needed

Method

  1. Put two glasses, a mixing bowl and two of the stroopwafels in the freezer for 10 minutes.
  2. Make the caramel: boil the maple syrup in a small saucepan over medium heat for 2 minutes, until the bubbles turn large and slow. Off the heat, whisk in the butter, then the cream. Return it to low heat for 1 minute, whisking, add the salt and pour it into a small bowl to cool for 15 minutes.
  3. While the caramel cools, leave the ice cream on the counter for 5 to 10 minutes, until a spoon presses in easily.
  4. Chop the two cold stroopwafels into pieces about 1 cm (½ inch) wide. Cut the third into quarters.
  5. Beat the ice cream in the cold bowl with a sturdy metal spoon for 30 to 60 seconds, until smooth and thick like soft serve. Add milk 1 tablespoon at a time only if it is too stiff to move.
  6. Add three quarters of the chopped stroopwafel and 2 tablespoons of caramel, fold 3 or 4 times, and spoon into the cold glasses. Top with the rest of the pieces and caramel and a stroopwafel quarter. Serve straight away.

Tools

  • Small saucepan
  • Whisk
  • Mixing bowl
  • Sturdy metal spoon
  • Chef's knife
  • 2 glasses or bowls

What You Need Before You Start

You need a freezer with room for two glasses and a bowl, a small saucepan and about 30 minutes, most of it waiting for the caramel to cool. There is no special equipment: a frozen bowl and a strong metal spoon turn store-bought ice cream into a soft-serve texture.

Cold is the one thing that decides the result. Every minute the ice cream spends in a warm bowl or under warm caramel turns it from thick to runny, so freeze the glasses and the bowl first, and let the caramel cool fully before it touches the ice cream.

Buy a full-fat vanilla ice cream rather than a light one or a frozen dessert. A rich, dense ice cream holds its shape when you beat it; a light, airy one turns into a milkshake quickly.

How to Get the Soft-Serve Texture

The texture comes from three steps done in order: soften, beat, fold. Skip the softening and you get hard lumps; beat too long and it runs; fold too much and the caramel disappears into beige.

Soften it on the counter

Leave the tub out for 5 to 10 minutes. It is ready when a spoon presses into the center without force but the ice cream still holds a scoop shape. A warm kitchen gets there sooner, so check at 5 minutes.

Beat it in a frozen bowl

Press and drag the ice cream against the side of the cold bowl with the back of a metal spoon for 30 to 60 seconds. It turns smooth, glossy and thick, the way soft serve falls off a cone. A stand mixer with the paddle on low does it in 20 to 30 seconds. Stop as soon as it is smooth.

Fold, do not stir

Add the stroopwafel pieces and caramel, then lift and turn the ice cream over them three or four times. You want ribbons of caramel and pockets of cookie in every spoonful.

The Stroopwafels: Why the Cookie Matters

The cookie is most of the flavor, so use real stroopwafels rather than plain waffle cone. A stroopwafel is two thin baked waffle layers held together by a syrup filling, and its caramel taste carries into the ice cream around each piece.

Ours are made in Canada, and the filling is sweetened with pure maple syrup. The first ingredient is Canadian-grown flour, with butter, Canadian eggs and cane sugar, and no artificial flavors. That maple filling is the reason this dessert tastes of maple rather than plain caramel.

Chill the stroopwafels before you chop them. Ten minutes in the freezer firms the filling so the knife cuts clean squares instead of bending the waffle. The pieces soften in the ice cream within a few minutes, which is why they go in last and the dessert is eaten straight away.

Two other sizes work. The mini stroopwafels come 15 to a 128 g box and only need halving, and the caddies hold 35 individually wrapped stroopwafels if you make this often. If you want to bake the cookies yourself, our Dutch stroopwafel recipe covers the iron, the dough and the filling.

The Maple Caramel

The caramel is maple syrup boiled for two minutes, then finished with butter and cream. Boiling drives off some of the water in the syrup, so it thickens into a sauce that clings to the ice cream instead of sinking to the bottom of the glass. The butter adds richness, the cream turns it opaque and soft, and the pinch of salt keeps it from tasting flat.

Watch the bubbles. At first they are small and fast; after about two minutes they turn large and slow, and that is the moment to take the pan off the heat. The cream foams up when it goes in, so pour it slowly and keep whisking. The sauce looks thin while hot and thickens as it cools, which takes about 15 minutes.

Use a dark, robust maple syrup if you have the choice. Its stronger flavor still tastes of maple after boiling and next to the sweetness of the ice cream; the difference between the grades is color and strength of taste, explained in our guide to maple syrup grades.

In a hurry, skip the caramel and pour a tablespoon of maple syrup straight from the bottle over each glass. It is thinner and runs to the bottom, but the maple taste is the same.

Homemade vs the Drive-Thru Version

The homemade cup swaps every part of the restaurant one for a version you control: ice cream for soft serve, whole stroopwafels for waffle cone pieces, and maple caramel for caramel sauce.

Five Ways to Change It

Keep the method and change one thing: the stroopwafel flavor, the ice cream or the form. Each of these uses the same quantities as the recipe card.

  • Chocolate: chocolate ice cream with our Chocolate Maple Stroopwafels, and the maple caramel as written.
  • Berry: vanilla ice cream with Strawberry Maple or Blueberry Maple Stroopwafels.
  • Matcha: vanilla ice cream with Matcha Maple Stroopwafels, and half the caramel so the tea taste comes through.
  • Milkshake: blend 2 cups (500 mL) of ice cream, ½ cup (125 mL) of milk and one stroopwafel for 10 to 15 seconds, then top with the caramel and the chopped pieces.
  • Coffee: pour a shot of hot espresso over each finished glass, the way a stroopwafel is eaten over a cup of coffee in the Netherlands.

Fixes for Common Problems

Almost every problem with this dessert is temperature. Here is what each one looks like and what to do.

It turned runny

The ice cream got too warm or was beaten too long. Put the glasses in the freezer for 10 minutes, then serve. Next time, stop beating as soon as it is smooth.

It has hard lumps

It was not softened enough. Give it another 2 to 3 minutes on the counter before beating, rather than adding more milk.

The caramel is too thin

It needed more boiling, or it is still warm. Chill it for 5 minutes. Next time, boil the syrup until the bubbles turn large and slow.

The caramel went stiff

It boiled too long or sat in the fridge. Warm it for 10 seconds in the microwave and stir in a teaspoon of cream, then let it cool again.

The cookie went soft

The pieces sat in the ice cream too long. Fold them in at the very end, and keep a few pieces back for the top.

The cookie bent instead of cutting

The filling was warm. Freeze the stroopwafels for 10 minutes and use a sharp knife with one firm press.

Making It Ahead

Make the caramel first, up to a day ahead, and keep it covered in the fridge. It sets firm when cold, so leave it out for 15 minutes or warm it for 10 seconds before using it.

The assembled dessert is best eaten within minutes. For a party, beat and fold each batch just before serving, or spoon the finished cups into the freezer and serve them within the hour, before the stroopwafel pieces soften.
