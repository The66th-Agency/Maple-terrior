Maple Syrup Cocktails: 8 Recipes, the Maple Old Fashioned First

The maple old fashioned is the place to start: 2 oz (60 mL) of bourbon or rye, ¼ oz (7.5 mL) of pure maple syrup and 2 dashes of bitters, stirred in the glass. Below it are seven more maple syrup cocktails, from a whiskey sour to a paloma, each in oz and mL, each credited to the bartender, writer or producers' group that published it, and each with the grade of maple syrup that suits it.

A maple syrup cocktail uses pure maple syrup where a bartender would otherwise reach for sugar or simple syrup. The syrup sweetens the drink and adds its own caramel and wood notes. The eight recipes below pair it with whisky, rum, apple brandy, tequila and coffee, and a little goes further than simple syrup does.

The eight recipes here come from the bartenders, food writers and producers who published them, and each card names its source. We set each one out in our own words, added millilitres beside the ounces and added the grade of syrup we would pour. We have not run a tasting of our own, so the measures are the authors', not ours.

For the grade alone: a Dark maple syrup suits seven of the eight, and a Golden syrup suits the bright, fruit-led paloma. The reasons are under Which Maple Syrup Is Best for Cocktails.

How we worked the numbers: millilitres are rounded at 30 mL to the ounce (one US fluid ounce is 29.57 mL, by the US National Institute of Standards and Technology). Standard drinks are the spirit's volume times 0.40, divided by 17.05 mL, the definition on Health Canada's page on alcohol. A bottle stronger than 40 percent counts for more, and liqueurs are left out.

1

Maple Old Fashioned

1 drinkMakes
StirredIn the glass
Rocks glassOne large cube
DarkGrade to pour

Ingredients

  • 2 oz (60 mL)bourbon, or rye for a drier drink
  • ¼ oz (7.5 mL)pure maple syrup, Dark, such as the syrup in our 100 mL maple leaf bottle
  • 2 dashesAngostura bitters
  • 1 striporange peel, and a maraschino cherry if you like
  • 1large ice cube

Method

  1. Pour the bourbon, maple syrup and bitters into a rocks glass and stir until the syrup has disappeared into the whisky.
  2. Lower in the large ice cube. The author adds the ice last because the syrup is hard to stir in around a big cube.
  3. Add the orange peel, and the cherry if you are using one.

No large cube? The author's answer is 3 or 4 ordinary ice cubes. She prefers one big cube because it melts more slowly and waters the drink down less.

Where this recipe comes from. The measures and method are from Maple Old Fashioned by Kristen Stevens of The Endless Meal, who writes her recipes from her kitchen in Squamish, BC. She writes that rye is a little less sweet than bourbon and still works well.

How Much Maple Syrup Goes in an Old Fashioned

We set side by side five recipes from the pages Google shows for maple syrup cocktails and the maple old fashioned, and their maple differs by a factor of six. Most land between ¼ oz and ⅓ oz, which is why the recipe above sits there.

Start at ¼ oz, taste, and add a little at a time. The low end, Ali Martin's half teaspoon, lets the whisky lead; the classic International Bartenders Association recipe uses a single sugar cube for 45 mL of whisky. The high end is a rum version, which brings us to the variation.

The maple rum old fashioned. Allen Katz's version on Liquor.com swaps the whisky for 2 oz (60 mL) of dark rum, doubles the maple to ½ oz (15 mL) and keeps the 2 dashes of Angostura. The recipe stirs it with ice and strains it into a chilled cocktail glass, finished with an orange twist.

2 to 4. Maple Sours

A sour is spirit, citrus and a sweetener shaken with ice. Maple takes the sweetener's place, and these three show how far the balance can move, from Kathryne Taylor's adjustable sour to the tart Maple Leaf.

2

Cinnamon Maple Whiskey Sour

Shaken · over fresh ice · Dark

  • 1½ oz (45 mL)bourbon
  • 1 oz (30 mL)fresh lemon juice
  • 2 to 4 tsp (10 to 20 mL)pure maple syrup, to taste; the author uses 3 tsp (15 mL)
  • 1 pinchground cinnamon, optional

Shake everything with ice in a cocktail shaker or a mason jar, then strain it into a glass over fresh ice. Leave out the cinnamon for a classic maple whiskey sour.

From Cinnamon Maple Whiskey Sour by Kathryne Taylor, Cookie and Kate. She notes the recipe can be multiplied up to four times in one shaker.

3

Maple Leaf

Shaken · served up · Dark

  • 2½ oz (75 mL)Canadian whisky
  • 1½ oz (45 mL)lemon juice
  • ½ oz (15 mL)pure maple syrup

Shake the three with ice until the shaker is cold, then strain into a chilled cocktail glass with no ice and serve it straight away. With three parts lemon to one of maple, it has the most lemon of the three sours.

From Paul Kushner's maple cocktails list on MyBartender, the only recipe here built on Canadian whisky.

4

Applejack Rabbit

Shaken · coupe · Dark

  • 2 oz (60 mL)bonded apple brandy
  • ¾ oz (22.5 mL)lemon juice
  • ¾ oz (22.5 mL)orange juice
  • ½ oz (15 mL)pure maple syrup, Dark

Shake with ice and strain into a chilled coupe, then finish with a lemon twist.

Jim Meehan's recipe, published as Applejack Rabbit on Liquor.com. It asks for grade B maple syrup, the old American name for the darkest syrup; our guide to maple syrup grades says a Very Dark or, for a slightly milder drink, a Dark syrup stands in for it.

5 to 7. Long Drinks Over Ice

Soda water, grapefruit and mint stretch these three into taller, lighter drinks. The paloma is the one place on this page where we would pour Golden syrup.

5

Autumn Rickey

Built · highball · Dark

  • 2lime wheels
  • 1½ oz (45 mL)bourbon
  • 1 oz (30 mL)lime juice
  • ½ oz (15 mL)pure maple syrup, Dark
  • To topclub soda

Fill a highball glass with ice and add the lime wheels. Pour in the maple syrup, lime juice and bourbon, top with club soda and stir briefly.

Dominic Venegas's recipe, published as Autumn Rickey on Liquor.com, which also asks for grade B syrup.

6

Maple Paloma

Shaken · topped with soda · Golden

  • 2 oz (60 mL)pink grapefruit juice
  • 1 oz (30 mL)lime juice
  • 1 oz (30 mL)tequila or mezcal
  • ½ oz (15 mL)pure maple syrup, Golden
  • 1 tbsp (15 mL)maple sugar for the rim, optional
  • 2 oz (60 mL)sparkling or soda water
  • 1grapefruit slice, halved, to garnish

Rim the glass with maple sugar. Shake the juices, tequila and maple with ice for about 20 seconds, strain into the glass, top with the soda and add the grapefruit.

From Maple Paloma by Marie Asselin, Food Nouveau. She encourages trying it with mezcal if you have some.

7

Maple Mojito

Muddled and shaken · highball · Dark

  • 8 to 10mint leaves, and a sprig to finish
  • 2 oz (60 mL)white rum
  • 1 oz (30 mL)lime juice
  • ½ oz (15 mL)pure maple syrup, Dark
  • 2 oz (60 mL)sparkling maple water

Muddle the mint gently in the bottom of a highball glass. Shake the rum, lime and maple with ice for 10 seconds, strain over fresh ice in the glass, top with the sparkling maple water and add the mint sprig.

From the Maple Mojito published by Maple from Canada, which asks for dark syrup "for its robust flavour" and gives a non-alcoholic rum for an alcohol-free version. Its metric measures give the maple as 12 mL.

8

Maple Bourbon Espresso Martini

Shaken hard · coupe · Dark

  • 2 oz (60 mL)bourbon
  • 1½ oz (45 mL)espresso, or cold brew
  • 1 oz (30 mL)coffee liqueur
  • ¾ oz (22.5 mL)pure maple syrup, Dark
  • 1 pinchsea salt
  • 1 dashground cinnamon
  • 3espresso beans

Brew the espresso and let it cool for a couple of minutes. Put everything but the espresso beans in a shaker with ice and shake hard for 30 to 40 seconds to build the foam, then strain into a coupe. For a finer foam, the recipe suggests double straining, through the shaker's strainer and a fine sieve. Finish with three espresso beans.

From Paul Kushner's maple cocktails list on MyBartender. Maple from Canada's own maple espresso martini asks for an amber syrup; we sell Golden and Dark, and Dark is the one our guide to maple syrup in coffee picks for espresso and milk drinks, because it comes through the coffee.

Which Maple Syrup Is Best for Cocktails

Use pure maple syrup, in the grade that matches how strongly you want to taste the maple. All four of Canada's color classes are Canada Grade A and meet the same rules; they differ in color and in how strong the maple tastes. We sell the lightest and the third, and each has its place behind the bar.

These are our own picks, worked out from what our coffee guide and our page on the two grades say each grade does, and not from a tasting. Either grade will make every drink here; the grade decides how much you taste the maple. Pancake or table syrup will not: it is mostly corn syrup with flavoring, and our guide to pure maple syrup vs table syrup shows how to tell them apart on the label. Maple Syrup in Place of Simple Syrup

Maple syrup is sweeter than the simple syrup most cocktail recipes call for, so use less of it. Canada Grade A maple syrup has to be 66 to 68.9 percent sugar and other dissolved solids, under the rules in the Canadian Food Inspection Agency's grade standards. Simple syrup is sugar dissolved in an equal volume of water, and rich simple syrup is two parts sugar to one of water, as Liquor.com defines them.

By our arithmetic, with a cup of granulated sugar at 198 g (King Arthur Baking's weight chart) and a cup of water at about 237 g, simple syrup comes out a little under half sugar by weight, and rich simple syrup a little under two thirds, against 66 to 68.9 percent dissolved solids for maple. So swap maple syrup for rich simple syrup measure for measure, and for plain simple syrup start with about two thirds of the amount, then taste. Maple also brings its own flavor, which simple syrup does not, so a drink can take a touch less than the sugar alone suggests.

Mixing a Batch for a Party

The old fashioned is the simplest to batch, because it has no juice or soda to add at the last minute. Kristen Stevens stirs the bourbon, maple and bitters together in a pitcher ahead of time, then pours each drink over ice when guests arrive. The pitcher holds no ice, so each drink takes its water from the ice in the glass. Multiplied by 8, her recipe is 16 oz (480 mL) of bourbon, 2 oz (60 mL) of maple syrup and 16 dashes of bitters; that multiplication is ours. For the whiskey sour, Kathryne Taylor shakes up to four drinks at once in one shaker.

Without the Alcohol

Maple from Canada's mojito recipe gives the swap: a non-alcoholic rum in place of the white rum, with everything else the same. The paloma also works without tequila, as grapefruit, lime, maple and soda over ice; that version is our idea, not Marie Asselin's. Use the same maple as in the full drink, and offer a glass of water beside any of these.

Storing an Opened Bottle

An unopened bottle keeps for years somewhere cool and dark. Once it is open, it belongs in the fridge: our guide to how long maple syrup lasts gives about a year for an opened glass bottle in the fridge, because syrup left warm can grow mold on top.
