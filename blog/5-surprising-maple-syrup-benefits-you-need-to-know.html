Is Maple Syrup Healthy?

What a tablespoon of pure maple syrup holds, how its sugar compares with the daily limit, white sugar and honey, and what studies in people have and have not shown.

By Kai Lytton 10 min read
Published March 5, 2025 Last updated November 6, 2026

The short answer

Pure maple syrup is a sugar first. A 15 mL tablespoon holds about 12 grams of sugar and 56 calories, and the World Health Organization counts syrups among the free sugars it asks people to limit. It does carry something white sugar lacks: a tablespoon gives about a third of a day's manganese, plus small amounts of riboflavin, zinc and plant compounds. Used in place of another sugar, in the same small amount, it is a reasonable swap. Poured on top of what you already eat, it is more sugar.

What Is in a Tablespoon of Maple Syrup

Health Canada's Canadian Nutrient File lists prepackaged maple syrup at 270 calories and 58.6 grams of sugar per 100 grams, almost all of it sucrose, the same sugar as white table sugar. It has no fat, protein or fiber. The table turns those figures into the spoonfuls people actually pour, and sets each nutrient against the daily values used on Canadian Nutrition Facts tables.

Manganese is the standout: one tablespoon covers about a third of the daily value and two cover about two thirds. Riboflavin follows at 12 percent a tablespoon. Everything else is a few percent at most, so maple syrup is a minor source of zinc, potassium, calcium and iron next to the foods that supply them in useful amounts. The sugar line matters more, because it grows at the same pace as the minerals.

How Much of Your Daily Sugar Limit Is in a Tablespoon

The World Health Organization's sugars guideline (March 4, 2015) asks adults and children to keep free sugars under 10 percent of their daily energy, and says a further cut to under 5 percent, roughly 25 grams a day, would bring extra health benefits. Its definition of free sugars includes the sugars naturally present in honey and syrups, so pure maple syrup counts in full.

Two tablespoons reach 24 grams, which is the whole of the lower WHO level. That is the honest frame for the rest of this page: whatever else maple syrup contains comes packaged with its sugar.

Maple Syrup vs White Sugar vs Honey

To compare the three sweeteners from one source, the table uses USDA FoodData Central, the American nutrient database, per tablespoon. A tablespoon of each weighs a different amount, which is part of why honey comes out higher.

Spoon for spoon, maple syrup and white sugar deliver almost the same sugar, and maple syrup has 4 more calories. The difference is in the minerals: white sugar has close to none, and maple syrup has measurable manganese, calcium, potassium and zinc. Honey carries more sugar and calories per tablespoon than either, and fewer of these minerals than maple syrup. Our maple syrup vs honey comparison covers taste and cooking as well.

Maple Syrup and the Glycemic Index

The glycemic index ranks how fast a carbohydrate food raises blood sugar, on a scale up to 100, tested against glucose as the reference food. In a 2014 answer on glycemicindex.com, Professor Jennie Brand-Miller of the University of Sydney put pure maple syrup at 54. On the same site's scale, 55 or less is low and 70 or more is high, which leaves 56 to 69 in the middle.

Fifty-four sits one point inside the low range, so maple syrup is a low glycemic index food on paper. The index measures speed, not amount. Two tablespoons still bring 27 grams of carbohydrate, and the total amount of sugar eaten matters more to blood sugar than which sugar it is.

What Studies on Maple Syrup Show

Most of the research on maple syrup and health happened in laboratories, on cells and concentrated extracts. One controlled trial in people has been published. Here is each level of evidence, from the weakest to the strongest.

Lab

Compounds found in the syrup

In 2011, the University of Rhode Island reported 54 compounds in pure maple syrup, five of them never found in nature before. One, quebecol, forms while sap boils into syrup. A 2022 review in the International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health lists the phenolic compounds and lignans identified since. Finding a compound in syrup shows it is there, not that a spoonful of syrup does anything in the body.

Lab

Extracts tested on cells and roundworms

In 2016, teams at the University of Toronto and the University of Rhode Island tested a maple syrup extract on brain-cell proteins linked to Alzheimer's disease, in test tubes and in tiny roundworms. The American Chemical Society release (March 14, 2016) quotes the lead researcher calling the work "preliminary laboratory-based". An extract is a concentrate, not the syrup on your table, and no study has tested whether eating maple syrup protects the brain.

People

One eight-week trial in 42 adults

In a 2024 trial in The Journal of Nutrition, Morissette and colleagues gave 42 adults with overweight and mild cardiometabolic changes two eight-week periods in random order. In one, maple syrup replaced 5 percent of their daily calories from added sugars. In the other, a flavored sucrose syrup did. Neither the volunteers nor the researchers knew which was which. Compared with the sucrose period, the maple syrup period showed:

Lowerblood sugar response in a glucose tolerance test
-7.8 gchange in abdominal fat, against +67.6 g with sucrose syrup
-2.7 mm Hgchange in systolic blood pressure, against +0.9 with sucrose syrup

Blood fats did not change. The trial swapped one sugar for another at the same amount; it did not add maple syrup to anyone's diet. It is one small, short trial, and the paper reports it was funded by the federation of maple producers in Quebec and that province's agriculture ministry.

Who Should Be Careful With Maple Syrup

  • People managing diabetes or blood sugar. Maple syrup raises blood sugar like other sugars, with about 14 grams of carbohydrate a tablespoon. Count it as carbohydrate and ask your doctor or dietitian how it fits your plan.
  • Anyone cutting sugar for weight or teeth. The WHO guideline rests on the link between free sugars and weight gain and tooth decay, and maple syrup is a free sugar.
  • Children. The WHO limits apply to children as well as adults. They are a share of daily calories, so a child who eats fewer calories than an adult has a smaller sugar allowance.

How to Use Maple Syrup Well

  1. Swap it in, do not add it on. The one human trial replaced other sugars with maple syrup. Pouring it on top of a diet that already has sugar adds sugar.
  2. Measure the first few pours. A tablespoon is 15 mL. Pour onto a spoon a few times and you will know what a free pour really holds.
  3. Bake with the right amount. Swapping sugar for syrup changes the liquid in a recipe. Our sugar to maple syrup conversion tool gives the amounts.
  4. Choose by taste, not by grade. Every grade has the same sugar range, so no grade is healthier. Our guide to maple syrup grades explains what the color classes do measure.

Questions About Maple Syrup and Health

Is maple syrup healthy?

Pure maple syrup is a sugar first: a 15 mL tablespoon holds about 12 grams of sugar and 56 calories in the Canadian Nutrient File. It also carries about a third of a day's manganese and small amounts of riboflavin, zinc and plant compounds that white sugar lacks. In place of another sugar, in the same small amount, it is a reasonable swap. Added on top of what you already eat, it is more sugar.

Is maple syrup healthier than sugar?

Spoon for spoon, they carry almost the same sugar: 12.1 grams in a tablespoon of maple syrup against 12.5 grams in white sugar, in USDA FoodData Central. Maple syrup adds manganese, calcium, potassium and zinc, which white sugar almost entirely lacks. One eight-week trial in 42 adults found small improvements when maple syrup replaced other added sugars, but a single small, industry-funded trial does not prove a health benefit.

Is maple syrup healthier than honey?

A tablespoon of maple syrup has 52 calories and 12.1 grams of sugar against 64 calories and 17.2 grams in a tablespoon of honey, in USDA FoodData Central, and more manganese, calcium and potassium. Honey is heavier per spoonful, which explains part of the gap. The World Health Organization counts both as free sugars to limit.

Is maple syrup anti-inflammatory?

That has not been shown in people. Laboratory studies have tested maple syrup extracts and compounds such as quebecol on cells, which is a long way from eating syrup. The 2024 trial in people reported, as an exploratory finding, fewer gut-bacteria functions linked to an inflammatory signal, which is not the same as showing less inflammation in the body.

Is maple syrup good for gut health?

The evidence is thin. In the 2024 trial in The Journal of Nutrition, the maple syrup period came with a drop in one group of gut bacteria, Klebsiella, which the researchers treated as an exploratory finding. Pure maple syrup has no fiber in the Canadian Nutrient File.

Can people with diabetes eat maple syrup?

Maple syrup raises blood sugar like other sugars and brings about 14 grams of carbohydrate a tablespoon. Its glycemic index of 54 is just inside the low range, but the amount eaten matters more than the index. Anyone managing diabetes should count it as carbohydrate and ask their doctor or dietitian how it fits. This page is not medical advice.

Is pure maple syrup allowed on a no-sugar diet?

No. Pure maple syrup is about 59 percent sugar by weight in the Canadian Nutrient File, almost all of it sucrose, the same sugar as white table sugar. Pure and natural describe where the sugar came from, not how much there is.

How much maple syrup is too much?

The World Health Organization recommends keeping free sugars, which include syrups, under 10 percent of daily calories, and says a cut to under 5 percent, roughly 25 grams a day, brings extra benefits. One tablespoon of maple syrup, about 12 grams of sugar, uses about half of that 25 grams before any other sugar in your day.

Is dark maple syrup healthier than golden?

The grades do not measure nutrition. Every grade has to hold 66 to 68.9 percent sugar solids, so every grade is mostly sugar. Our guide to maple syrup grades explains what the color classes do measure.
