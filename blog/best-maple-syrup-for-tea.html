The Best Maple Syrup for Tea

Maple syrup in tea works best when the grade matches the cup: a light syrup for teas you drink without milk, a darker one for tea with milk, spice or smoke. Here is which grade to use in black, green and herbal tea, how much to stir in, and how maple compares with honey and sugar, teaspoon for teaspoon.

By Maple Terroir 8 min read
Published November 23, 2026 Last updated November 23, 2026

Which Maple Syrup for Which Tea

The rule of thumb is the one in our guide to maple syrup in coffee: where the drink leaves the sweetener exposed, use Golden; where milk or a strong flavor sits between you and the syrup, use Dark. The milk and water columns below come from the UK Tea and Infusions Association's brewing table, which says which teas are drunk with milk and how hot the water should be. The grade column is our recommendation.

How Much Maple Syrup to Put in Tea

Start with one teaspoon in a 250 mL cup, taste, and add more from there. If you already take one teaspoon of sugar, one teaspoon of maple syrup gives you about the same amount of sugar: a level teaspoon of maple syrup carries about 4.0 g of sugars, and a teaspoon of white sugar about 4.2 g, by the figures in the chart.

Honey is the outlier. Honey holds less water than maple syrup, 17 g in every 100 g against 32 g for maple in USDA's figures, so a teaspoon of honey carries more sugar, about 5.7 g. To match one teaspoon of honey with maple syrup, use about one and a half teaspoons.

For a delicate green tea, start with half a teaspoon of Golden. For a large mug of chai or a tea latte with plenty of milk, one to two teaspoons of Dark is a fair place to begin. Stir while the tea is hot. If you take milk, steep the tea first, take the bag out, then add the syrup and the milk.

Maple Syrup vs Honey vs Sugar in Tea

Teaspoon for teaspoon, maple syrup sits between white sugar and honey on calories and nearly level with sugar on sugars. What changes most in the cup is the flavor and the kind of sugar. Nearly all the sugar in maple syrup is sucrose, the same sugar as the white kind (58 of 60 g per 100 g, USDA), while honey's is mostly fructose and glucose. Maple syrup also carries some manganese: about 0.19 mg in a teaspoon, where USDA lists almost none for sugar or honey. All three still sweeten the cup with sugar, so none of them turns tea into a health drink.

Two Maple Teas to Make at Home

One hot cup with Dark and one cold glass with Golden. Both use the brewing times above and stir the maple in while the tea is still hot. Maple from Canada's maple sugar iced tea recipe works the same way: the maple goes into the hot tea before the cold water.

Maple Tea Latte

An Earl Grey or Ceylon tea with steamed milk, sweetened with Dark. Makes one mug.

  1. Steep one bag of Earl Grey or Maple Ceylon Tea in 150 mL of water at 90 to 98°C for 3 to 4 minutes, then take the bag out.
  2. Stir in one to two teaspoons of Dark maple syrup while the tea is hot.
  3. Steam or froth 100 mL of milk, or a plant milk, and pour it over the tea. Taste, and add a little more maple if you want it sweeter.

Maple Iced Green Tea

A light green tea with lemon, sweetened with Golden. Makes two glasses.

  1. Steep two green tea bags in 250 mL of water at about 80°C for 2 to 3 minutes, then take the bags out.
  2. Stir in two teaspoons of Golden maple syrup while the tea is hot, so it mixes in evenly.
  3. Add 250 mL of cold water and the juice of half a lemon, then pour over ice. Taste, and stir in more maple only if it needs it.

Maple from Canada's Maple Sugar Iced Tea (maplefromcanada.com, read November 23, 2026) steeps four green tea bags in one cup of boiling water with half a cup of maple sugar for five minutes, then adds cold water and the juice of one lemon, for six portions. Our two methods are our own.

"Maple tea" can mean two different cups. One is any tea with maple syrup stirred in, the subject of this guide. The other is a tea blended with maple before it reaches you, which smells of maple from the moment the water goes in and is barely sweet until you add something.
