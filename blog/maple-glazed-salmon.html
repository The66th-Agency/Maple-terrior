Maple Glazed Salmon: A Five-Ingredient Maple Syrup Salmon Glaze

This maple syrup salmon glaze takes maple syrup, soy sauce, Dijon mustard, garlic and black pepper, whisked in one bowl. Brush it on, bake at 400°F for 12 to 15 minutes, brush again, and four people eat in under half an hour.

By Maple Terroir October 19, 2026 9 min read

Maple syrup and salmon work because each fixes what the other lacks. Salmon is rich and mild, and the syrup's sugar caramelizes on its surface into a glossy crust, while soy sauce and mustard add the salt and sharpness that keep the glaze from tasting like dessert.

Below is the recipe card, then everything around it: what each ingredient does, how to tell the fish is done, four other ways to cook it, which maple syrup makes the best glaze, what to serve with it, and how to store leftovers.

Maple Glazed Salmon

Prep10 min
Bake12 to 15 min
Serves4
Oven400°F (200°C)

Ingredients

  • 4 salmon fillets, about 170 g (6 oz) each, skin on or off
  • ¼ cup (60 mL) pure maple syrup
  • 2 tablespoons soy sauce
  • 1 tablespoon Dijon mustard
  • 1 garlic clove, grated
  • ¼ teaspoon black pepper, plus salt to taste
  • 1 green onion, thinly sliced, to finish
  • Lemon wedges, to serve

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 400°F (200°C) and line a baking tray with parchment.
  2. Whisk the maple syrup, soy sauce, Dijon, garlic and pepper in a small bowl. Pour a third of it into a second bowl and set it aside for the end.
  3. Pat the salmon dry, set it on the tray skin side down, season lightly with salt and brush the tops with the glaze from the first bowl.
  4. Bake for 12 to 15 minutes, brushing once more halfway through, until the thickest fillet reaches 70°C (158°F).
  5. Brush on the glaze you set aside, scatter the green onion and serve with lemon.

What Each Ingredient Does

Every ingredient in the glaze has one job: the maple sweetens and browns, the soy salts, the mustard thickens and sharpens, and the garlic and pepper add depth. Knowing the jobs lets you swap an ingredient without breaking the balance.

Pure maple syrup

Use pure maple syrup, made only from boiled maple sap. Pancake or table syrup is mostly corn syrup with maple flavoring, and it turns thin and one-note in the oven; our guide to pure maple syrup vs table syrup explains how to read the label.

Soy sauce and Dijon

Soy sauce brings the salt, so season the fish lightly and taste the glaze before adding more. Dijon mustard does two things: its sharpness cuts the sweetness, and it holds the glaze together so it clings to the fish instead of running onto the tray.

The salmon

Choose fillets of an even thickness, about 2.5 cm (1 inch) at the thickest point, so they finish at the same time. Skin-on fillets hold together better on the tray, and the skin slides off easily after baking if you would rather not eat it.

How to Make Maple Glazed Salmon, Step by Step

The method is three brushings of glaze around one short bake. The details below are the ones that decide whether the glaze turns glossy or burns.

Set some glaze aside before it touches the fish

Pour a third of the glaze into a separate bowl before you brush any raw salmon. The brush picks up juices from the raw fish, so the glaze in the first bowl is only for the tray; the clean bowl is the one you finish with at the table.

Dry the fish, then brush

Pat each fillet dry with paper towel so the glaze sticks instead of sliding off in a puddle. Brush a thin coat on the top and sides; a thick first coat burns before the fish is cooked.

Brush again halfway

At the six- or seven-minute mark, pull the tray out and brush on a second coat. Maple syrup is mostly sugar, so it browns fast at 400°F: dark edges are the glaze caramelizing, and if they darken before the center is done, lay a sheet of foil loosely over the tray for the last few minutes.

How to Tell When Salmon Is Done

Salmon is done when the thickest part reaches 70°C (158°F), the safe internal temperature for fish in Health Canada's cooking temperature chart. An instant-read thermometer pushed in from the side of the fillet gives the most reliable reading.

Without a thermometer, press the thickest part with a fork. Cooked salmon turns from translucent to opaque all the way through and separates into flakes along its natural lines.

Four Other Ways to Cook It

The glaze works with any method; what changes is when it goes on. The hotter and closer the heat, the later you brush, because the sugar scorches before the fish is done.

On the grill

Oil the grates and cook the fillets skin side down over medium heat with the lid closed. Brush the glaze on only for the last three or four minutes so the sugar does not burn onto the grates.

In a pan

Sear the fillets skin side down in a little oil over medium-high heat until the skin crisps, then flip, turn the heat to low and pour in the glaze. Spoon it over the fish as it bubbles and thickens, a minute or two.

In the air fryer

Line the basket with a parchment liner, brush the fillets and cook at 400°F, checking the temperature at eight minutes. Fillets thinner than 2.5 cm finish sooner.

Under the broiler

For a darker crust, bake as written, brush on the reserved glaze and finish under the broiler for one to two minutes. Stay at the oven door; the glaze goes from glossy to burnt quickly.

Which Maple Syrup Makes the Best Glaze

A dark, robust syrup makes the best salmon glaze, because its flavor holds up against soy sauce and mustard. A golden syrup works too and gives a lighter, sweeter glaze; the difference between the grades is color and strength of taste, explained in our guide to maple syrup grades.

Five Ways to Change the Glaze

Keep the maple and the soy, and change one other ingredient at a time. Each of these goes into the bowl before you set the reserved third aside.

  • Spicy: a teaspoon of sriracha or a pinch of chili flakes.
  • Ginger: a teaspoon of freshly grated ginger beside the garlic.
  • Orange: the zest of one orange and a tablespoon of its juice in place of the lemon.
  • Sesame: a teaspoon of toasted sesame oil in the glaze and sesame seeds on top.
  • Whole-grain mustard: in place of Dijon, for a glaze with texture.

What to Serve With Maple Salmon

Serve it with something plain to soak up the glaze and something green to cut the sweetness. Steamed rice and a quick vegetable cover both in the time the fish bakes.

  • Rice or roasted potatoes for the extra glaze on the plate.
  • Green beans, broccoli or asparagus, roasted on a second tray in the same oven.
  • Maple roasted carrots for a full maple dinner; our list of maple syrup uses beyond pancakes has the method.
  • A green salad with a maple vinaigrette, one of the everyday uses for maple syrup most people skip.

Storing and Reheating Leftovers

Cooked salmon keeps 3 to 4 days in the fridge and 4 to 6 months in the freezer, according to Health Canada's food storage chart. Store it in a sealed container in a fridge at 4°C (40°F) or colder.

Reheat it gently, covered, in a low oven, so the fish warms through without drying out, or eat it cold, flaked over a salad or rice bowl. The glaze itself keeps in the fridge too; the maple syrup shelf life checker covers how long the bottle lasts once it is open.
