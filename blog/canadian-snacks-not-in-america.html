Canadian Snacks Not Sold in America

13 snacks sold in Canada that their makers do not sell in the US, from maple stroopwafels to Caramilk. Every one checked on the maker's own Canadian and US sites, or a US government page, on November 22, 2026.

The Short Answer

Canadian snacks their makers do not sell in America include Maple Terroir's maple stroopwafels, maple butter popcorn and maple sugar cookies, the Cadbury bars Caramilk, Mr. Big and Wunderbar, Kinder Surprise eggs and Maynards Swedish Berries. Most are missing because the maker leaves them out of its US range. Kinder Surprise is the exception: US food law keeps it out.

For a maple snack
Maple Terroir maple stroopwafels, made in Canada with a pure maple syrup filling, $8.99 a box, or the maple butter popcorn, $4.99 a bag.
For a chocolate bar
Caramilk, made in Toronto and missing from Hershey's US Cadbury range.
For something to share
Maple Terroir maple sugar cookies, 10 wrapped in a box, or Swedish Berries, made in Hamilton.
To leave out of a US-bound bag
Kinder Surprise, which the FDA can detain at the border.

Kinder Surprise

By Ferrero

Kept out by US food law, not by the maker.

Canada · kinder.com/caListed
US · FDA Import Alert 34-02Named in the alert

Kinder Surprise is a chocolate egg with a toy inside. Ferrero's Canadian page describes it as Kinder chocolate "with a surprise and toy in every single egg" and sells it as a 20 g egg, one at a time or in threes.

US food law treats candy with a non-food object partly or fully inside it as adulterated. The Food and Drug Administration's Import Alert 34-02, last published May 12, 2025, lets officers detain listed candy of this kind at the border without examining it, and the FDA says the alert was created when it was made aware of Kinder Surprise eggs. Ferrero sells Kinder Joy in the US instead, a scoopable treat that comes with a limited-edition toy.

Carrying Canadian Snacks Across the Border

Taking these snacks to someone in the US means carrying them. Here is what the two US agencies involved say.

Declare agricultural items
US Customs and Border Protection says all travellers must declare agricultural items, from meat and fruit to plants and seeds, whether in checked bags, carry-on bags or a vehicle, and that a CBP agriculture specialist may inspect them.
Know the penalty
CBP says failing to declare a prohibited agricultural item can bring a civil penalty of up to $1,000 for a first offence in non-commercial amounts, and the item is taken.
Leave Kinder Surprise at home
Kinder Surprise is the egg an FDA import alert names, so do not pack it.
Check before you pack
CBP sends travellers to the US Department of Agriculture for which foods are allowed. Neither CBP page names candy or cookies, so check before a trip rather than assume.

Sources: CBP's pages Bringing Agricultural Products Into the United States and Agricultural Items, and FDA Import Alert 34-02, read November 22, 2026.

How We Checked

We started from the snacks that eight of the pages ranking for this search name most, added the maple snacks they leave out, and kept only the ones we could check at the source on November 22, 2026. For each, we opened the maker's Canadian page to confirm it is sold in Canada, then the maker's US site or brand list, or a US government page, to see whether it is sold there.

A maker's US list can change, and a snack can turn up in a US import shop. So "not sold in America" here means the maker does not offer it in the US, or US rules keep it out, on the date we checked. Maple Terroir's own three entries rest on its own store, which sells in Canada and runs no US store. For more Canadian food and the companies behind it, see Canadian food products by kind.
