The Best Canadian Snacks, Ranked for 2026

Twelve Canadian snacks scored on five things you can check: whether they are made with pure maple, whether they are made in Canada, whether the maker names Canadian ingredients, how well they travel and whether you can buy them from the maker. Maple Terroir stroopwafels, maple popcorn and maple cookies take the top three places.

By Maple Terroir11 min read
Published November 6, 2026Last updated November 12, 2026

The Short Answer

The best Canadian snack to buy is a maple one: Maple Terroir stroopwafels, made in Canada with Canadian-grown flour and a pure maple syrup filling, score 10 out of 10, with the same maker's maple popcorn and maple cookies beside them. The most popular Canadian snacks are ketchup chips, All Dressed chips, Coffee Crisp and Jos. Louis, going by nine Canadian snack lists.

How We Scored Them

We took the packaged snacks the nine lists name most often, added three Canadian-made snacks the lists missed (Swedish Berries, Purdys Hedgehogs and Caramilk) and three Maple Terroir snacks, and checked each one on its maker's website on November 6, 2026. Dishes and bakery treats such as poutine and butter tarts are covered separately below, because you eat them where they are made.

How often the lists name a snack is shown in the chart above and used only to order snacks with the same score. We did not run a taste test, so taste is not scored. Every score comes from what a maker or a named source publishes, which is what you can check yourself.

Made with pure maple

2 points if pure maple syrup or pure maple sugar is an ingredient, per the maker, 0 if it is not.

Made in Canada

2 points if the maker says it is made in Canada, 1 if only a retailer or an independent source says so, 0 if we found no statement.

Canadian ingredients

2 points if the maker names Canadian-grown ingredients, 1 if the maker says "domestic and imported" or only an independent source names them, 0 if not stated.

Travels well

2 points for a shelf-stable snack with no chocolate coating, 1 for chocolate or a soft cake that can melt or crush, 0 if it needs a fridge.

Buy from the maker

2 points if the maker sells it online in Canada, 1 if it is sold in grocery stores, 0 if we found neither.

Maple Syrup Stroopwafels

10/ 10

Maple Terroir. $8.99 for a 136 g pack, made in Canada.

A stroopwafel is two thin waffle layers pressed around a caramel filling. Maple Terroir fills its stroopwafels with maple syrup from one farm in Quebec, and its product page lists Canadian-grown flour as the first ingredient, with Canadian eggs and no margarine or glucose-fructose. Set one on top of a hot cup of tea or coffee for a minute and the filling softens.

Maple Terroir stroopwafels were supplied to the TED Conference in Vancouver in 2024, 2025 and 2026. For a smaller bite, the mini stroopwafels come 15 to a box at $8.99.

04

Ketchup Chips

6/ 10

Lay's (PepsiCo Canada) and Old Dutch. Named by 5 of the 9 lists, more than any other snack.

Ketchup chips are the snack Canadians name first: five of the nine lists we read include them, more than any other snack. The Canadian Encyclopedia says Hostess sold most of them in the 1970s and 1980s, and that nobody knows for certain who made them first.

Lay's says its ketchup chips are "Made in Canada from domestic and imported ingredients." Old Dutch, a family-owned company that started in Winnipeg in 1954, says it makes its chips for the Canadian market in four facilities across Canada. Both are grocery store chips; neither maker sells online.

Hawkins Cheezies

6/ 10

W.T. Hawkins Ltd., Belleville, Ontario. Named by 3 of the 9 lists.

The maker's site lists bags from 28 g to 285 g and multipacks, with no online shop, so you buy them in grocery and convenience stores.

Vachon's own Jos. Louis page names no place of manufacture, so the score for that criterion comes from the encyclopedia. It is a soft cake that crushes easily, sold in grocery stores in six-packs.

Canadian Treats to Eat in Canada

Four of the most-named treats are fresh foods rather than packaged snacks, so they are not scored above. They are worth eating while you are here, or baking at home.

Butter tarts

Named by 4 lists. Small pastry tarts with a sweet, buttery filling. The Canadian Encyclopedia says the first published recipe appeared in 1900, in a cookbook from the Women's Auxiliary of the Royal Victoria Hospital. To bake them at home, the maple butter tarts recipe uses pure maple syrup in the filling.

Poutine

Named by 4 lists. Fries, cheese curds and gravy. The Canadian Encyclopedia says the combination first appeared in rural snack bars in the late 1950s. It is a hot dish, so it is a snack to eat in Canada rather than pack.

BeaverTails

Named by 3 lists. Fried pastries made from a Hooker family recipe. Pam and Grant Hooker first sold them at the Killaloe Craft and Community Fair in 1978, according to the company's own history, and today they are made fresh in BeaverTails shops.

Nanaimo bars

Named by 2 lists. A no-bake chocolate square. The Canadian Encyclopedia dates the first version to a 1952 Nanaimo Hospital cookbook; the City of Nanaimo calls its origin "elusive."

Choosing Canadian snacks to send or bring home

Pick snacks that are wrapped, shelf-stable and hard to crush. Stroopwafels, popcorn, wrapped cookies, gummies and Cheezies all hold up in a bag; chips crush, and chocolate bars and Jos. Louis cakes melt or squash in the heat. If the label matters to the person, check that the maker says the snack is made in Canada: of the nine other snacks here, eight carry that claim from the maker or an independent source.

For a ready-made gift, Maple Terroir maple gift sets put stroopwafels, popcorn, shortbread and syrup in one box, and a list of the best Christmas gift basket companies in Canada compares eight companies that send gift baskets. Canadian Snack Questions

Ketchup chips. Five of the nine Canadian snack lists on the first page of Google Canada name them, more than any other snack. All Dressed chips, Coffee Crisp, Jos. Louis, butter tarts and poutine follow with four lists each.

The makers say so for Maple Terroir stroopwafels, popcorn and cookies, Lay's and Old Dutch ketchup chips, Hawkins Cheezies (Belleville, Ontario), Ruffles All Dressed, Coffee Crisp (Toronto), Maynards Swedish Berries (Hamilton), Purdys Hedgehogs and Caramilk (Toronto). The Canadian Encyclopedia says Vachon still makes Jos. Louis in Canada.

Wrapped, shelf-stable snacks without chocolate: maple stroopwafels, maple popcorn, wrapped maple cookies, Swedish Berries and Cheezies. Chips can crush, and chocolate bars and soft cakes such as Jos. Louis can melt or squash.

Maple Terroir sells its maple stroopwafels, popcorn, cookies and nuts online and ships to all ten provinces, free on orders of $99 or more. Purdys sells its chocolates online. The chips, chocolate bars and candy on this list are sold in grocery and convenience stores rather than by their makers.
