How to Choose a Stroopwafel

Start with how you will eat them. A stroopwafel is two thin waffle layers pressed around a syrup filling, and the usual way to eat one is to rest it on a hot cup of coffee or tea until the filling softens.

Then check the price per piece and the price per 100 g together. A mini at 60 cents looks cheap until you see that four minis weigh the same as one full-size stroopwafel. The chart puts all ten on the same measure.

How We Scored Them

We listed stroopwafels that a Canadian seller or the brand itself offered online on November 16, 2026, and read each pack's price, size and details on that page. Each of five things a buyer can check scores 0, 1 or 2 points, for 10 in all.

We did not run a taste test, so taste is not scored. A pack with a low score can still be the right one for you: the scores reward packs you can order today, that come wrapped one by one and that say where they are made.

Made in Canada
2 points if the brand or its packaging says it is made in Canada, 1 if only a seller says so, 0 if it is imported, not stated, or the pack and the seller disagree.
Wrapped one by one
2 points if each stroopwafel has its own wrapper, 1 for a pouch of two or a tin that closes again, 0 for one bag or box, or where the page does not say.
Price for the weight
2 points under $2.50 per 100 g, 1 point from $2.50 to $4.99, 0 at $5.00 or more. We use weight because a mini and an extra-large stroopwafel are different sizes.
In stock online
2 points if it was in stock online on the check date at a store that ships to the provinces, 1 if it was in a store only, 0 if it was sold out. A buyer can only order what is in stock.
Organic, vegan or gluten-free
2 points for a certified organic or vegan stroopwafel or a gluten-free one, 0 otherwise. There is no middle score here.
Ties
Entries with the same score are listed by price per 100 g, lowest first. The Maple Terroir box of four and minis share one entry, placed by the box of four's price.
