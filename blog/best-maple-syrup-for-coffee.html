Why Maple Syrup Belongs in Your Coffee

A liquid sweetener dissolves the moment it meets a hot cup. Stir maple syrup into coffee and it disappears into the liquid with no grit left at the bottom and no waiting for granules to break down. That solves the grit you get from granulated sugar. You taste the sweetness evenly from the first sip to the last.

Then there is the ingredient list. Pure maple syrup contains one thing, the boiled-down sap of a maple tree. There is no refined white sugar, no caramel color, and no artificial flavoring. When you stir Maple Terroir into your cup, you are adding syrup from one sugar bush, sold by the family that started Maple Terroir in 1978. Our Golden syrup is certified organic by Ecocert, Canada Organic and USDA Organic. People who want to know exactly what goes into their morning cup get a short, honest answer.

Maple also brings flavor that white sugar cannot. Sugar adds sweetness and nothing else. Maple adds sweetness plus soft notes of caramel, vanilla, and butterscotch that sit naturally alongside the roasted character of coffee. The two come from the same warm, toasted family of flavors, so they meet in the middle rather than fighting each other.

That flavor also changes with the grade you reach for, which is why coffee drinkers get to tune the cup. A lighter grade keeps the sweetness quiet and lets the coffee lead. A darker grade pushes the maple forward so you taste it through milk and a bolder roast. The two grades let you match the maple to the coffee you drink. The sections below map each Maple Terroir grade to the cup it suits best.

Which Grade for Which Coffee

Dark for Lattes, Cappuccinos and Darker Roasts

Dark is our robust, late-harvest grade, the syrup in our maple leaf bottles and jug. A latte or a cappuccino puts a lot of milk between you and the espresso, and a softer syrup gets lost in it. Dark comes through the milk and stands up to a darker roast. If you keep one bottle by the coffee machine for milk drinks, make it this one.

Golden for Black Coffee and Light Roasts

Golden is our delicate, early-harvest grade, the syrup in our organic bottles. Black coffee and light roasts leave the sweetener exposed, so a gentle maple has room to show. Stir a teaspoon into a light roast and the maple stays in the background.

Cold Brew and Iced Coffee

Cold drinks are where a liquid sweetener really pulls ahead. Granulated sugar refuses to dissolve in a cold cup and settles into a clump at the bottom. Maple syrup is already liquid, so it folds straight into cold brew or iced coffee. Stir it into the coffee before you add ice. Dark holds its own over ice and in strong cold brew; Golden suits a lighter cold brew. A Simple Maple Latte

Pull or brew the coffee

Pull a double shot of espresso, or brew a small, strong cup of coffee. You want it on the stronger side, since the milk will soften it.

Stir in the maple

Add one to two teaspoons of Dark maple syrup to the hot coffee and stir until it dissolves. Doing this while the coffee is hot means the maple melts in evenly before the milk goes anywhere near it.

Steam the milk and pour

Steam or froth your milk of choice and pour it over the sweetened coffee. Finish with a light drizzle of maple on top if you want the aroma to greet you before the first sip. For a softer maple note, use Golden instead.
