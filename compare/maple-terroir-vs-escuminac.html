Two Single-Farm Syrups

Escuminac was set up in 1998 by Martin Malenfant, a third-generation syrup maker, on a 500-hectare stand of old maples at Escuminac in the Gaspé. It now runs 65,000 taps for organic maple syrup and 5,000 for birch sap, and is certified carbon neutral (Escuminac, A know-how).

Every bottle of ours comes from one farm in the Appalachian Mountains, run by the same family business since 1978. With either brand you know which forest your syrup came from, which a blended grocery syrup cannot tell you. Our terroir page explains how the forest and the season shape the taste.

Three Harvests or Two Grades

Escuminac splits its season three ways. Extra Rare comes from the first days of the run and is the mildest. Great Harvest is the main season and its most popular syrup. Late Harvest comes from the end of the season and is intensely dark, with notes the brand describes as rye and roasted sugar (Extra Rare, Great Harvest, Late Harvest).

We sell two: Golden from early in the season for pouring, and Dark from late in the season for baking and glazes. If you want to taste how syrup changes across one spring, Escuminac's three bottles show more of it. If you want one light and one dark, our two cover that. Our guide to maple syrup grades explains the color scale.

Organic Across the Range

All of Escuminac's maple syrup is certified organic, the dark Late Harvest included, and has been since 2006. Our Golden is certified by Ecocert, Canada Organic and USDA Organic, and our certifications page explains each one. Our Dark, in the maple leaf bottle, is not certified organic. If you want a dark syrup that is organic, Escuminac's Late Harvest is one.
