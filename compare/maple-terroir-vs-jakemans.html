Maple Terroir vs Jakeman's Maple Syrup

Maple Terroir bottles syrup from one farm in two grades: a certified organic Golden, from $14.99 for 250 mL, and a Dark in a maple leaf bottle. Jakeman's maple syrup is an Ontario blend from more than 200 farms, sold as a Grade A Amber, from $12.99 for 250 mL. Both are pure maple syrup with nothing added. Choose Maple Terroir to know which forest your syrup came from. Choose Jakeman's for the lowest price per liter.

Is Jakeman's Maple Syrup Good?

Yes. Jakeman's is real maple syrup and a sound bottle for everyday pouring. Its website lists no additives or preservatives and sells "Grade A quality only". The one grade its website names is a Grade A Amber, which Jakeman's calls "the perfect balance of flavor". The Jakeman family has made maple syrup in Ontario since 1876, and the business is in its fifth generation (jakemansmaplesyrup.com, read November 24, 2026).

What Jakeman's does not give you is a single origin. Its syrup is blended from more than 200 farm partners. Maple Terroir bottles syrup from one farm in two grades, Golden and Dark, and its Golden carries three organic certifications.

So the answer depends on what you want from the bottle. For a steady Amber at a low price per liter, Jakeman's is good syrup. To taste one forest, or to pour something lighter or darker than Amber, Maple Terroir is the better fit.

One Farm or Many Farms

Every bottle of Maple Terroir syrup comes from one forest on one farm. Sap changes with the trees, the soil and the weather of each spring, so a single farm's syrup carries that one place and that one season. The terroir page explains what shapes the taste, and why early syrup comes out golden and late syrup dark.

Jakeman's buys syrup from producers across Ontario and blends it. Its website describes "more than 200 local farm partners", among them Mennonite communities in St. Jacobs, with the syrup "refined and bottled" at its facility in Beachville, Ontario (Jakeman's homepage and its article on the family's history, read November 24, 2026). Blending many farms gives a steady taste from bottle to bottle.

Organic Certification

Both brands sell a certified organic syrup. Maple Terroir's Golden grade is certified by Ecocert, Canada Organic and USDA Organic, and the certifications page explains who runs each certification. The Dark grade in the maple leaf bottle is not certified organic.

Jakeman's offers a certified organic maple syrup alongside its classic syrup, and the article on its website that says so does not name the certifying body (read November 24, 2026). On October 19, 2026 its organic page listed 250, 370 and 500 mL glass, with the 500 mL at $23.99 and the 250 mL sold out.

Grades and Taste

Canada's Grade A maple syrup comes in four color classes, from lightest to darkest: Golden, Amber, Dark and Very Dark. Maple Terroir sells the first and the third, from the same farm. Golden comes from early in the season and has a delicate taste for pouring. Dark comes from late in the season and has a robust maple flavor. The guide to maple syrup grades explains the scale.

The one grade Jakeman's website names is an Amber, the class between them. If you want to choose between a light syrup and a dark one, Maple Terroir's two bottles give you that choice.

Which One Suits You

Choose Maple Terroir if

  • You want syrup from one farm, the same forest in every bottle.
  • You want certified organic syrup in a 250 mL bottle for $14.99.
  • You want to choose between an early-season Golden and a late-season Dark.
