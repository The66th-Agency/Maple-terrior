The Pantry Staple

What Maple Sugar Actually Is

Maple syrup with the water taken all the way out. What remains is a dry, fine sugar that pours and measures like any other, with the taste of the syrup it came from.

01

Maple Syrup, Boiled Further

Maple sugar is made by boiling pure maple syrup past the syrup stage, until almost all the water is gone, then stirring it as it cools until it sets into fine, dry granules. One ingredient, start to finish.

02

A One-for-One Sweetener

Maple sugar measures cup for cup like white sugar, so it drops into almost any recipe without conversion. It dissolves in hot and cold, which is why it works in coffee, tea, batters, and rubs.

03

Pure Maple, Nothing Refined

Cane sugar is stripped down to plain sweetness. Maple sugar keeps the colour and the deep, round flavour of the sap it came from, so a small spoonful carries real maple character.

In the Kitchen

How to Use Maple Sugar

Anywhere you reach for white or brown sugar, you can reach for this instead.

Swap It One for One

Use maple sugar in place of white or brown sugar at a one-to-one ratio in most recipes. No conversion, just a deeper, maple-led sweetness.

Stir Into Coffee and Tea

It dissolves hot or cold, so it sweetens coffee, tea, and cocktails with maple flavour and none of the stickiness of pouring syrup.

Finish and Bake

Sprinkle it over crepes, yogurt, oatmeal, and ice cream, or bake it into cookies, glazes, and rubs where you want maple to lead.

Sugar or Syrup

Maple Sugar vs Maple Syrup

Both start as the same sap from the same trees. The syrup is boiled until it pours. The sugar is boiled further, until it dries and granulates.

Maple Syrup

Pourable and liquid. Reach for it to top pancakes and yogurt, to glaze, or to sweeten anything you want to drizzle.

Maple Sugar

Dry and granulated. Reach for it to measure, sprinkle, and bake, anywhere a recipe calls for sugar by the cup or spoon.
