What Is a Stroopwafel?

Stroopwafel is Dutch for syrup waffle: two thin layers of baked dough held together by a caramel syrup filling. Bakers in Gouda, in the Netherlands, first made them in the late 18th or early 19th century, according to Wikipedia.

Ours keep the Dutch shape and change the syrup. The filling is sweetened with pure maple syrup, and the first ingredient is Canadian-grown flour.

To eat one the Dutch way, set it on top of a hot cup of coffee or tea for a minute, so the steam warms the waffle and softens the filling. To bake your own, follow our stroopwafel recipe. For a dessert with no baking, chop two into vanilla ice cream for our homemade stroopwafel McFlurry.

Maple Stroopwafel FAQ

What is in a Maple Terroir stroopwafel?

The label on a box of Pure Maple lists wheat flour, sugars (glucose syrup, cane sugar and pure maple syrup), sustainable palm oil, butter, liquid whole eggs, soy flour, oat fiber, natural maple flavour, sea salt, baking soda and soy lecithin. The flour and eggs are Canadian. Every stroopwafel in our caddies is individually wrapped.

Do Maple Terroir stroopwafels use real maple or artificial maple flavor?

Real maple. The filling is sweetened with pure maple syrup, and the label adds a natural maple flavour, with no artificial flavoring.

Are the stroopwafels gluten-free?

No. The stroopwafels are made with Canadian wheat flour and are not gluten-free. The label says they contain milk, eggs, wheat and soy. Our Pure Maple Syrup Butter Popcorn is made with gluten-free ingredients, according to its product page. Check the label if you have a severe allergy.

No. The stroopwafels are not certified organic. They are sweetened with pure maple syrup.
