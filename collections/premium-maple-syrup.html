How to Read a Maple Syrup Label

The color and taste on a maple syrup label describe the syrup's flavor; they do not rank its quality. In 2014 Canada replaced its old Canada No. 1, No. 2 and No. 3 grades with Canada Grade A, in four color classes from Golden to Very Dark, and Canada Processing Grade for syrup that does not meet Grade A, so a dark syrup and a golden one can carry the same grade. Our guide to maple syrup grades walks through every color.

Our labels name two: the organic syrups are Golden, Delicate Taste, from early in the season, and the maple leaf bottles are Dark, Robust Taste, from late in the season.

Which one to pour depends on the cup or glass. Our guide to the best maple syrup for tea puts Golden in green and herbal tea and Dark in black tea with milk, and our maple syrup cocktails pair each recipe with its grade, Dark for whisky and rum and Golden for a paloma.

Before You Order Maple Syrup

Where the syrup comes from, which grade to pick and how to store it.

Where does your maple syrup come from?

From one farm, the same farm for both grades. Sap from early in the season becomes the Golden, and sap from late in the season becomes the Dark. Our terroir page explains why the two taste different.

Is your maple syrup pure maple syrup?

Yes. Every bottle we sell is pure maple syrup with no additives, preservatives or coloring. Table syrup, the cheaper pancake syrup in grocery stores, is a different product, often a corn syrup blend with caramel color and added flavor; our guide to pure maple syrup vs table syrup explains how to tell them apart.

Is your maple syrup certified organic?

Our organic syrups are. The Organic Pure Maple Syrup and the Organic First Tap Nouveau are certified organic by Ecocert, Canada Organic and USDA Organic. The maple leaf bottles are not organic, and the certification does not cover our stroopwafels, cookies, chocolates or other treats.

What grade of maple syrup do you sell?

Our labels say Canada Grade A. The Organic Pure Maple Syrup and the Organic First Tap Nouveau are Golden, Delicate Taste. The maple leaf bottles are Dark, Robust Taste. Each product page shows its label.

Should I buy the Golden or the Dark?

Choose by what you will pour it on. Golden has a light, delicate maple flavor for ice cream, fruit, cheese or a light stack of pancakes. Dark has the stronger maple flavor, so it still comes through in baking, coffee and glazes. Our guide to the best maple syrup for baking goes further.

How should I store maple syrup?

Keep a sealed bottle in a cool, dark cupboard. Once it is open, keep it in the fridge: the University of Maine Cooperative Extension advises refrigerating maple syrup after opening so it does not grow mold. Our guide to how long maple syrup lasts covers each kind of bottle.

Can I buy maple syrup by the case or wholesale?

Yes. Every bottle comes in packs of 12, 24, 36 and 48, from 10 to 25 percent off; pick one in the price table above or on the product page. Stores, cafes and gift shops buying to resell can see our maple syrup wholesale page or email info@mapleterroir.com.

The maple leaf bottles: glass bottles in the shape of a maple leaf, sold as a souvenir gift from Canada in 50, 100 and 250 mL. The 50 mL bottle costs $7.99, or $7.20 each in a pack of 12, for wedding favors and stocking stuffers. Our maple syrup gift sets pair syrup with maple treats.
