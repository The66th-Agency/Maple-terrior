How to use the calculator.

Three numbers and two choices. The order updates as you change them.

  1. Count the people

    Count the people who will eat them. Some guests will not. For gifts, count one person for each gift.

  2. Set the days

    For an office or a home, use the days the order should last. For one event, leave it at 1.

  3. Choose how many each

    The calculator starts at one a day. Raise it for a dessert table, where people take more than one.

  4. Pick the size and the flavors

    Full-size stroopwafels are 34 g. Mini stroopwafels are bite-size, 15 to a box. A mix spreads the caddies across the flavors in stock.

  5. Add the order to the cart

    Each line has its own button, and one button adds the whole order. You can still change any quantity in the cart.

How the order is worked out.

Stroopwafels needed = people × days × stroopwafels each per day

The calculator then checks every combination of the packs in stock and keeps the one with the lowest total that reaches your number. A mix of flavors is counted in whole caddies only, spread across the flavors in stock. Total ÷ stroopwafels in the order gives the price each. The saving is the cost of enough single boxes to cover the same number, minus your total.

  • One each per day is an assumption. The box label counts one 34 g stroopwafel as a serving, so the calculator starts there. It is a starting point. Set what your group will eat.
  • Packs are whole. The order can come to a few more than you need, and the result shows how many are left over.
  • Cheapest comes first. When two orders cost the same, the calculator keeps the one with more stroopwafels.
  • Savings come from the prices charged. Each one is worked out from the store's prices, measured against single boxes.
  • Flavors set the packs. Boxes of four come in maple syrup flavor only. The pack of eight caddies is one flavor, so a mix uses it only when one flavor reaches eight caddies.
  • Sold-out packs are left out. Prices and stock are read from the store when the page loads. If that read fails, the prices of November 16, 2026 are shown.
  • It counts pieces only. The calculator stops at 10,000 and does not check how long a pack keeps. For a larger order, or one meant to last many months, ask us first.

Ordering questions.

How many stroopwafels per person should I order?

Start from one each per day, the serving size on the box label, and change it to fit your group. The calculator multiplies people by days by stroopwafels each, then finds the cheapest packs that cover the total.

How many stroopwafels are in each pack?

A box of four holds 4 full-size stroopwafels of 34 g each. A box of minis holds 15 bite-size pieces. A caddy holds 35 full-size stroopwafels, individually wrapped.

What is the cheapest way to buy stroopwafels for a group?

For full-size stroopwafels, by the caddy. At the prices read on November 16, 2026, a caddy of 35 works out to $1.14 a stroopwafel, against $2.25 in a single box of four, and eight caddies of one flavor bring it to $1.03.

Can I mix flavors in one order?

Yes. A single caddy can be any of the five flavors: Pure Maple, Chocolate Maple, Blueberry Maple, Strawberry Maple or Matcha Maple. The pack of eight caddies is one flavor.

Should I order minis or full-size stroopwafels?

By weight, four minis equal one full-size stroopwafel: a mini is about 8.5 g and a full-size one is 34 g. Choose by how you will serve them, then set how many each to match.

How far ahead should I order for an event?

Orders ship to every Canadian province in 3 to 9 business days, so allow for that before your date. For Christmas 2026, order by December 10.
