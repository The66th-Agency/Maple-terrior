I want to know

The ratio comes from the Rule of 86: divide 86 by the sap's sugar percentage. Cornell's beginner guide gives the same figures, about 43 gallons of sap at 2 percent sugar and 86 gallons at 1 percent. A sap refractometer or hydrometer gives the reading for your own trees.

Why 86.

Finished maple syrup is 66 to 67 percent sugar, according to Cornell's beginner guide. The Rule of 86 is the shortcut producers use to get there from sap.

The rule

Divide 86 by the sap's sugar percentage. Sap at 2 percent gives 43, so 43 gallons of sap boil down to one gallon of syrup.

Sweeter sap, less boiling

Every half point of sugar matters. Sap at 3 percent needs about 29 gallons for a gallon of syrup, a third less wood or gas than sap at 2 percent.

What changes the sugar

Species first, then the tree itself. A maple with a large, healthy crown in full sun tends to give sweeter sap than a crowded one. Cornell's beginner guide says the same.

When to stop

Syrup is finished at 7°F above the boiling point of water, according to Ohio State's hobby guide. Past that it crystallises; short of it the syrup is thin and can spoil.

Ratio questions.

What is the sap to syrup ratio?

About 43 to 1 for sugar maple sap at 2 percent sugar. Divide 86 by the sugar percentage for any other sap.

How much sap makes a quart of syrup?

A little over 10 gallons of 2 percent sap makes one quart.

Which tree gives the best ratio?

Sugar maple and black maple, at 2 percent or higher. Red and silver maples run lower, and birch is far lower.

How do I measure sap sugar?

With a sap refractometer, which reads in degrees Brix, or a sap hydrometer. One degree Brix is one percent sugar.
