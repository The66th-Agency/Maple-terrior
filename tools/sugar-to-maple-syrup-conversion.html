The Kitchen Science

Why the recipe changes.

Maple syrup is a liquid sweetener, so a straight one-for-one swap throws a recipe off. Four small changes bring it back.

The ratio

Maple producers land on three quarters of a cup of syrup for every cup of white sugar. The swap keeps the recipe sweet without flooding it with extra liquid.

The water

About one third of maple syrup is water. That is why the recipe gives up 2 to 4 tablespoons of its other liquids, so the batter keeps the texture the baker intended.

The heat

The sugars in maple syrup brown faster than white sugar. Dropping the oven by 25°F keeps the edges from darkening before the middle bakes through.

The baking soda

The association also recommends 1/4 to 1/2 teaspoon of baking soda when the recipe includes no acidic ingredient of its own, such as buttermilk or sour cream.

The rules on this page follow the guidance the Vermont Maple Sugar Makers' Association publishes for home bakers.
