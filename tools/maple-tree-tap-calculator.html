How many taps can your maple take?

Measure the trunk, enter the number, and the calculator returns the safe number of taps and a rough guide to the sap and syrup that tree gives in a season.

I measured the

Measure 4.5 feet (about 1.4 metres) above the ground. A tape around the trunk gives the circumference; the calculator turns it into a diameter for you.

Safe number of taps

1 tap

About 1 quart of syrup. At 2 percent sugar, it takes about 43 gallons of sap to make a gallon of syrup.

Taps by trunk size.

The same rule the calculator uses, measured at chest height. When in doubt, one tap is always the safer choice.

The bands follow the Cornell Maple Program's beginner guide. Some guides, including a later section of the same Cornell notebook, allow a third tap above 25 inches; Ohio State's hobby guide caps every tree at two, and so does this calculator.

Why the numbers stay small.

A maple heals every taphole it is given. Tappers keep to these limits so the tree stays healthy and keeps giving sap for generations, as Cornell's beginner guide puts it.

The stained wood

Each hole leaves a column of stained, non-conducting wood inside the trunk. Too many holes in a small trunk and the tree runs out of clean wood to tap.

The 10 inch floor

A trunk under 10 inches across is still putting its sugar into growth. Waiting a few seasons costs a little syrup now and keeps the tree for decades.

The healthy crown

Size is not the only test. Skip a tree with a thin or dying crown, a damaged trunk, or one that was stressed by drought or defoliation last summer.

The fresh hole

Drill a new hole each season, away from old ones, and pull the spout when the run ends. A clean hole closes over in about two years, according to Cornell's beginner guide.

Tapping questions.

How many taps can one maple tree support?

One tap for a trunk 10 to 17 inches across, two for 18 inches and up, and never more than two. A tree under 10 inches should not be tapped yet.

How big does a maple need to be to tap?

At least 10 inches in diameter at chest height, which is a circumference of about 31 inches. Cornell's beginner guide puts the first tap at 10 to 12 inches.

How much sap does one tap give?

6 to 10 gallons in an average season, according to Ohio State's hobby guide. Cornell's beginner guide puts a tree in a forest at about 10 gallons. The calculator uses 10.

How much syrup is that?

About a quart. Sugar maple sap near 2 percent sugar takes roughly 43 gallons to boil down to one gallon of syrup.
